  - Limited investment is directed toward a defensible balance of service, risk, value, and readiness.
  - Decision criteria and evidence can be understood by leaders, practitioners, oversight bodies, and communities.
  - Portfolio choices account for dependencies and impacts across asset systems and programs.
people:
  - Service and program owners
  - Asset managers and engineers
  - Finance and budget leaders

flow:
  - Establish shared decision criteria, definitions, weighting approach, and review authority.
  - Develop candidate investments with scope, outcomes, evidence, cost, timing, dependencies, and readiness.
  - Compare candidates consistently while retaining important qualitative context.
  - Review portfolio balance, funding constraints, delivery capacity, geographic effects, and cross-project dependencies.
  - Record decisions and assumptions, then revisit them as conditions, funding, or evidence change.
architecture: >-
  Portfolio decision support connects asset, condition, risk, service, cost, project, geographic,
  and funding information while retaining the source and timing of each input. Scenario tools can
  support comparison without replacing the recorded decision process.
ai: >-
  Recommendation or scenario assistance can help teams explore combinations and surface relevant
  evidence. Authorized leaders retain investment authority, and the criteria, assumptions,
  uncertainty, and material exclusions remain reviewable.

Prioritization is how an agency turns a larger set of valid needs into a manageable portfolio. A transparent process helps people understand why one investment advances before another and how the overall portfolio supports public outcomes.
