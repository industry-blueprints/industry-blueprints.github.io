  - Leaders can see the likely scale and timing of renewal needs across the portfolio.
  - Forecasts connect technical needs to service outcomes, risk, affordability, and delivery capacity.
  - Assumptions and information confidence remain visible when plans are compared.
people:
  - Asset manager
  - Engineer or lifecycle planner
  - Finance and budget staff
  - Capital program manager

flow:
  - Establish planning horizons, asset strategies, cost bases, treatment choices, and forecasting assumptions.
  - Combine inventory, age, condition, criticality, service, work, cost, and project information at an appropriate level of detail.
  - Model likely maintenance, rehabilitation, replacement, improvement, and retirement needs over time.
  - Test funding, timing, service, and delivery scenarios and record the assumptions behind them.
  - Refresh the forecast as assessments, costs, projects, service expectations, and operating experience change.
architecture: >-
  Forecasting draws from operational asset records, condition and inspection history, financial
  systems, project portfolios, cost libraries, GIS, and analytical models. The architecture keeps
  source observations distinct from planning assumptions and calculated scenarios.
ai: >-
  Predictive methods may support selected forecasts when sufficient comparable history exists.
  Planners review model fit, uncertainty, changed conditions, and policy assumptions before using
  the result in a capital outlook.
---

A capital-needs forecast is a planning view rather than a promise that every modeled project will be funded. It helps an agency understand the long-term shape of stewardship needs and explore the consequences of different timing and funding choices.
