## Business definition
To make and amend binding regulations through a procedure that gives the public a genuine
opportunity to be heard, produces a record adequate to defend the result, and yields text the
regulated community can actually comply with.

**Distinct from [policy development](/capabilities/policy-development/).** Policy is what the
organization decides; rulemaking is the formal, often statutorily prescribed procedure by which
certain decisions acquire the force of law. Confusing the two produces rules made in the wrong
process and vulnerable on that basis alone.

## Desired outcomes

- Statutory authority for the rule confirmed and cited before drafting
- Procedural requirements met exactly — notice, comment period, response, publication
- Significant comments genuinely considered and the reasoning recorded
- A complete, retrievable docket for every rule
- Compliance obligations clear enough to be met without legal advice
- Rules reviewed periodically rather than accumulating indefinitely

## Lifecycle
Authority analysis · regulatory agenda planning · impact and burden analysis · drafting · internal
and legal clearance · notice publication · comment intake and docketing · comment analysis and
response · final text and effective date setting · publication and codification · guidance
issuance · periodic review and repeal

## Domain model
Rule register with authority citations and status · docket of comments and their disposition ·
impact analyses and assumptions · version history and codification references · effective and
compliance dates · guidance documents linked to the rules they interpret · petitions for
rulemaking

## Level variance

- **Federal.** Formal notice-and-comment rulemaking under administrative procedure requirements,
  with a public docket, an obligation to respond to significant comments, and judicial review of
  both substance and procedure.
- **State.** State administrative procedure acts with analogous requirements that vary
  substantially — comment periods, legislative review committees, and small-business impact
  analyses differ by jurisdiction and must be checked rather than assumed.
- **County / municipal.** Ordinances and administrative regulations adopted in public session
  under open meeting law. The binding procedural constraint is more often the meeting notice
  requirement than a comment period.

## What to get right

**Get the procedure exactly right.** Notice too short, a comment period miscounted, a significant
comment unaddressed — any of these can void a rule whose substance is otherwise sound.

**Analyse every significant comment, not just a sample.** Volume is a real constraint — thousands
of submissions against a short deadline — but a response written from a sample is what a
procedural challenge targets first.

**Keep the docket as a retrievable record.** Comments in an inbox and disposition in someone's
spreadsheet won't be there two years later when it is needed — a real docket will.

**Give the regulated community lead time before the compliance date.** Learning of an obligation
on the day it takes effect guarantees non-compliance that then gets enforced against — building in
an interval avoids that.

**Use guidance to interpret rules, not to replace them.** Substantive obligations issued as
guidance to avoid the procedure move faster, but they're just as easily reversed for the same
reason.

**Build in sunset and periodic review.** Rules that accumulate for decades need resourcing to
repeal, because repeal means accepting the risk the rule was made for — the same accretion
dynamic seen in [grants](/capabilities/grants-management-outbound/) compliance requirements.

retention, and an auditable history. Applicable controls vary with the workload, jurisdiction,
and information sensitivity; a dedicated trust model has not yet been developed for this draft.
