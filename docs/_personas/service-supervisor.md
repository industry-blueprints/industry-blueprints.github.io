## Role

The Service Supervisor is accountable for the conditions in which a team delivers service. They
balance demand, capacity, risk, quality, and policy; intervene in exceptions; and make or assign
decisions that require elevated authority. The role looks across individual matters without
replacing the ownership of coordinators and workers.

## Goals

- Recognize aging, blocked, sensitive, or poor-quality work early.
- Balance workload and capability across people and teams.
- Resolve escalations with a recorded rationale and clear follow-through.
- Improve service outcomes by finding patterns rather than blaming individual cases.

## Responsibilities

The supervisor reviews exceptions, approves or rejects controlled actions, reassigns work, manages
coverage, monitors outcome and equity measures, and investigates recurring failure modes. They
maintain separation of duties where an independent decision is part of the control model.

## Needs

The supervisory view connects aggregate measures to explainable queues and records. It exposes age,
ownership, blockers, target breaches, reopened work, unusual access, and quality signals while
respecting sensitive-case discovery rules. Trend information includes enough context to avoid
turning a policy target into an indiscriminate performance score.

## Friction

Common friction includes lagging reports, metrics detached from workload complexity, hidden work,
manual escalation tracking, unclear approval queues, and broad access granted merely because the
person is a manager. Alerts without prioritization make material exceptions harder to see.

## Variations

The role may be team lead, duty manager, service manager, program supervisor, escalation reviewer,
or quality lead. Approval authority, operational span, and direct involvement vary by organization
and matter sensitivity.
