## Role

The Service Coordinator turns accepted demand into owned, visible work. They interpret service
classifications and jurisdiction, establish the responsible queue or person, monitor progress,
resolve routine routing problems, and keep the Service Seeker informed across organizational
boundaries.

## Goals

- Put each matter with an accountable owner using a defensible routing decision.
- See aging, blockers, handoffs, and service commitments across the active queue.
- Resolve exceptions before they become invisible or repeatedly transferred.
- Communicate useful progress and a complete disposition.

## Responsibilities

The coordinator validates classification, records why responsibility was assigned, manages
referrals and reassignments, requests missing information, monitors targets, and confirms that a
resolution is ready for closure. Authority to approve, perform, or reverse specialist work remains
with the applicable role and policy.

## Needs

The workspace combines queue priorities, ownership, service rules, relevant interaction history,
blocked actions, target dates, and current contact status. Handoffs preserve their reason,
acknowledgment, and next owner. Views distinguish a policy target from a prediction.

## Friction

Common friction includes ambiguous ownership, stale directories, work hidden in personal inboxes,
unacknowledged referrals, inconsistent classifications, targets without exception context, and
status dashboards that do not reveal the next action. Cross-team visibility may also reveal more
personal information than coordination actually requires.

## Variations

The role may be called dispatcher, navigator, queue manager, service owner, case coordinator, or
triage specialist. Some organizations distribute coordination to delivery teams; others maintain a
central service function. Authority and span of control vary without changing the core need for
traceable ownership.
