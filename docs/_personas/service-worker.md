## Role

The Service Worker performs the substantive work behind a request or case. Depending on the
workload, that work may involve research, analysis, inspection, field service, repair, eligibility
review, correspondence, or a professional decision. The role is defined by responsibility for an
activity and its evidence, not by a particular job title.

## Goals

- Understand the requested outcome, relevant context, and limits of assigned authority.
- Carry out work safely and efficiently without reconstructing the entire history.
- Obtain clarification, evidence, access, or approval when needed.
- Record what happened well enough for coordination, audit, and communication.

## Responsibilities

The worker accepts or questions an assignment, performs authorized activities, protects sensitive
information, records material observations and decisions, raises blockers, and supplies completion
evidence. They distinguish work completed from a matter fully resolved when closure belongs to
another role.

## Needs

The work view provides the location or subject, expected result, relevant evidence, safety or policy
constraints, due context, contact path, and valid next actions. Mobile and intermittent-connectivity
support may be central for field work. The record separates verified facts from notes, predictions,
and AI-generated suggestions.

## Friction

Common friction includes incomplete assignments, duplicated systems, missing evidence, unclear
authority, unsafe or inaccessible locations, notification overload, and administrative capture that
competes with delivery. A single generic workflow often obscures the very different evidence and
decision needs of specialist work.

## Variations

The role includes field agents, caseworkers, investigators, analysts, inspectors, technicians,
program specialists, and partner personnel. Work may be individual or collaborative, scheduled or
event-driven, office-based or mobile, and advisory or decision-making.
