## Role

The Intake Worker is the human front door for phone, counter, correspondence, outreach, or assisted
digital service. They translate a conversation into a usable organizational record without forcing
every need into the same process. The role may be performed by contact-center staff, receptionists,
program staff, caseworkers, or service coordinators.

## Goals

- Understand the person's need and immediate context quickly.
- Capture an accurate account without making the person repeat it.
- Distinguish information, request, case, emergency, and out-of-scope paths.
- Set an honest expectation and complete a traceable handoff.

## Responsibilities

The Intake Worker confirms the requester's words and contact preferences, gathers the minimum facts
needed for the next decision, records the source, identifies urgent or sensitive handling, and
acknowledges what was accepted. They do not invent facts or promise an outcome owned by another
function.

## Needs

The workspace provides a concise intake guide, approved knowledge, duplicate and prior-contact
context, clear required facts, accessible communication tools, and visible routing choices. It
distinguishes a suggestion from a confirmed classification or decision.

## Friction

Common friction includes fragmented channel histories, long forms before the need is understood,
unclear service boundaries, duplicate data entry, unreliable directories, and performance measures
that reward short contacts over correct handling. Excessive access can expose unrelated sensitive
matters; insufficient access can cause avoidable repetition.

## Variations

Intake may be centralized or embedded in a program, generalist or specialist, synchronous or
asynchronous, and internal or partner-operated. In low-volume settings the same person may perform
intake, coordination, and fulfillment while the responsibilities remain distinct.
