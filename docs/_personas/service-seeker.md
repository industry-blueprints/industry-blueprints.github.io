## Role

The Service Seeker initiates or participates in an interaction because something needs to be
understood, delivered, corrected, or resolved. The role describes a relationship to a service, not
a permanent identity. The same person may be a resident, constituent, customer, applicant,
employee, patient, beneficiary, authorized representative, or anonymous reporter in a particular
workload.

## Goals

- Find the right path without understanding the organization's internal structure.
- Explain the need once and know it was understood.
- Learn what happens next, who is responsible, and when an update is likely.
- Provide additional information and receive an intelligible outcome.
- Maintain control over personal details, communication channels, and accessibility preferences.

## Responsibilities

The Service Seeker supplies the facts reasonably available to them, responds when clarification is
needed, and uses the available reference or account to follow the matter. Some contexts add duties
such as attestation, consent, timely response, or reporting a material change.

## Needs

The experience makes service scope, required information, current state, next action, and outcome
visible in plain language. It supports assisted and self-service channels, accessible formats,
preferred language, representation by another person, and recovery when identity or reference
information is unavailable.

## Friction

Common friction includes choosing between unfamiliar departments, repeating the same history,
opaque status labels, silent transfers, unexplained closure, inaccessible channels, and being asked
for information the organization already holds. Authentication can also exclude a legitimate
participant when it becomes the only route to service.

## Variations

The role may be anonymous, identified, authenticated, or represented by another authorized person.
It may initiate a simple request, participate in a longer-running case, or be affected by work that
someone else initiated. Workloads specialize the role with local terminology and obligations.
