## Overview

To ensure people have the knowledge and certification their role requires, develop capability the
organization will need, and prepare successors for the roles it is about to lose.

**Compliance training and capability development are different activities sharing a budget line.**
The first must be completed and evidenced; the second must change what someone can do. Managing
both to a completion metric guarantees the second is not achieved.

## Personas

- Human Resources Director
- Knowledge Manager

## Common Processes

1. Training requirement identification by role
2. curriculum and pathway design
3. scheduling and delivery
4. completion tracking and evidence
5. certification and license tracking with renewal prompts
6. continuing education credit management
7. tuition assistance administration
8. development planning
9. leadership and supervisory preparation
10. succession identification and readiness
11. effectiveness evaluation

## Outcomes and Measures

- Mandatory and role-required training completed within its window, and evidenced
- Certifications tracked with expiry, and renewal prompted before lapse
- Development aligned to capability the organization actually needs
- Learning available at the moment of need, not only in scheduled events
- Successors identified and prepared for roles at retirement risk
- Effect measured on performance, not on attendance

| Measure | Class |
|---|---|
| Mandatory training completed within its required window | Process |
| Certifications current, and lapses in the period | Process |
| Renewal prompted before expiry rather than after | Process |
| Critical roles with an identified, ready successor | Process |
| Internal promotion rate into supervisory roles | Outcome |
| Capability change demonstrated after development | Outcome |
| Training spend against identified capability gaps | Input |

**Successor readiness for critical roles is the measure that matters most**, because the
demographic exposure in government is unusually concentrated — and it is almost never reported.
