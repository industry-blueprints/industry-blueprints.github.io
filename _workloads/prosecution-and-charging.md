## Overview

To review referred cases, decide whether the evidence and the public interest support a charge and
which charge fits, meet disclosure obligations, and resolve cases through diversion, plea,
dismissal, or trial.

**The charging decision is the widest discretion in the system and the least examined.** It
determines exposure, bail posture, plea leverage, and collateral consequences that outlast the
sentence. It is made by an individual, usually quickly, usually with no requirement to record the
reasoning, and it constrains every actor downstream.

## Common Processes

1. Referral intake and screening
2. evidentiary sufficiency review
3. charging decision and charge selection
4. declination with recorded reason
5. diversion eligibility assessment and referral
6. bail position determination
7. disclosure identification, tracking, and production
8. witness and law enforcement credibility disclosure tracking
9. plea negotiation and offer policy
10. trial preparation and conduct
11. victim consultation and notification
12. sentencing recommendation
13. appeal and post-conviction response
14. conviction integrity review
15. case data reporting
