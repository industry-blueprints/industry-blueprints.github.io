## Overview

To make and amend binding regulations through a procedure that gives the public a genuine
opportunity to be heard, produces a record adequate to defend the result, and yields text the
regulated community can actually comply with.

**Distinct from policy development.** Policy is what the
organization decides; rulemaking is the formal, often statutorily prescribed procedure by which
certain decisions acquire the force of law. Confusing the two produces rules made in the wrong
process and vulnerable on that basis alone.

## Personas

- Program Manager
- Constituent
- Records Manager

## Common Processes

1. Authority analysis
2. regulatory agenda planning
3. impact and burden analysis
4. drafting
5. internal and legal clearance
6. notice publication
7. comment intake and docketing
8. comment analysis and response
9. final text and effective date setting
10. publication and codification
11. guidance issuance
12. periodic review and repeal

## Outcomes and Measures

- Statutory authority for the rule confirmed and cited before drafting
- Procedural requirements met exactly — notice, comment period, response, publication
- Significant comments genuinely considered and the reasoning recorded
- A complete, retrievable docket for every rule
- Compliance obligations clear enough to be met without legal advice
- Rules reviewed periodically rather than accumulating indefinitely
