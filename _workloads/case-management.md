## Overview

Case Management enables an organization to coordinate work around a matter that persists beyond a
single transaction. A case establishes a durable boundary for responsibility, participants,
activities, evidence, decisions, and outcome.

Use a case when work needs continuing ownership, controlled lifecycle, multiple activities, or an
auditable resolution. Do not create a case merely because a system needs somewhere to store a form.
A request may be resolved without a case, produce one case, contribute to an existing case, or—when
several independent matters are discovered—produce several cases.

## Personas

- Service Seeker
- Intake Worker
- Service Coordinator
- Service Worker
- Service Supervisor

## Common Processes

1. Receive and triage the matter.
2. Establish the case boundary, participants, owner, priority, and access.
3. Plan and complete activities, tasks, communication, and evidence gathering.
4. Review required decisions and approvals.
5. Record a structured resolution and communicate the outcome.
6. Close, reopen, reassign, or escalate the case when the work requires it.

## Outcomes and Measures

| Desired outcome | Useful measures |
|---|---|
| Explicit ownership | Cases with a named current owner; reassignment time |
| Timely progression | Age by lifecycle stage; time to resolution |
| An intelligible history | Cases with required activities, communication, and evidence recorded |
| A durable outcome | Closed cases with a structured disposition and rationale |
| Appropriate participant communication | Required notices completed; reopened cases caused by incomplete communication |
