## Overview

To take a person from accepted offer to productive contributor — completing pre-employment
requirements, establishing identity and access, issuing equipment, delivering required training,
and integrating them into the work.

**Onboarding is the most cross-functional process in the domain and is usually owned by nobody.**
HR, IT, facilities, security, payroll, and the hiring manager each hold a piece. Each piece works;
the sequence does not.

## Personas

- Human Resources Director
- Chief Information Officer

## Common Processes

1. Offer acceptance and pre-boarding
2. background check, fingerprinting, and clearance
3. credential and license verification
4. benefits enrollment and payroll setup
5. identity establishment and account provisioning
6. role-based access assignment
7. equipment and workspace preparation
8. orientation delivery
9. mandatory and role-specific training
10. probationary check-ins
11. early attrition review

## Outcomes and Measures

- Pre-employment requirements completed before the start date, not after
- Accounts, access, and equipment ready on day one
- Access granted by role, at the level the role requires and no more
- Mandatory training completed within its required window
- A named contact for the first weeks who is not the hiring manager alone
- Early attrition understood rather than absorbed

| Attrition within the first ninety days | Outcome |

Ninety-day attrition is the honest outcome measure. A person who leaves that fast usually
encountered something in the first weeks, and the cost of the whole
recruiting cycle is lost with them.
