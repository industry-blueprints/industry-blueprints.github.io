# Vision Summary & Implementation Plan

## Independent Advisory, Industry Blueprints, and Professional Platform

### Vision

Build an independent professional platform around **enterprise applications, business systems, architecture, modernization, and AI**—drawing on decades of experience while remaining deliberately vendor-independent.

The long-term goal is not simply to create a consulting company. It is to build a durable body of professional capital consisting of:

* Independent consulting and advisory services
* A strong personal professional brand
* Open industry knowledge and reusable frameworks
* Executable reference architectures and open-source software
* Articles, diagrams, presentations, and technical research
* YouTube and other educational content
* A professional network and audience
* Reusable intellectual property and consulting methodologies

This platform should complement a traditional career rather than initially depend on replacing it.

A full-time role can provide income, collaboration, interesting problems, and continued professional experience. The independent platform creates an asset that remains personally owned and can grow throughout future career changes.

The objective is therefore a **portfolio career** in which employment, consulting, open-source work, teaching, research, and creative technical work can coexist.

# Core Point of View

Several ideas can form the intellectual foundation of the platform.

### Software creation is becoming commoditized. Engineering judgment is not.

AI-assisted development, coding agents, configurable platforms, and increasingly capable development tools make software easier and faster to create.

That does not make important systems easier to engineer.

Organizations must still understand architecture, security, information models, integration, ownership, lifecycle management, maintainability, governance, and operational risk.

### Build versus buy is becoming a spectrum.

Modern organizations can combine packaged applications, configurable platforms, custom software, open-source components, generated software, APIs, automation platforms, and AI agents.

The architectural challenge is increasingly deciding **where each approach belongs**.

### Blueprint first, platform second.

Organizations should begin with the business problem, processes, information model, users, capabilities, constraints, and architecture.

Technology selection should follow.

A case-management system should first be understood as a case-management system—not merely as a Dynamics implementation, Salesforce implementation, custom application, or ServiceNow application.

### AI changes enterprise applications without eliminating the need for systems.

AI may radically change how users interact with enterprise applications and how applications are created.

Organizations will still require authoritative data, permissions, processes, transactions, auditability, integrations, and systems of record.

### Easier software creation increases the importance of governance.

If AI makes applications dramatically easier to create, organizations are likely to create significantly more software.

Architecture, lifecycle management, security, ownership, testing, and governance therefore become more important rather than less important.

### Enterprise AI is often an integration problem.

The language model is only one component.

Real enterprise value depends on connecting intelligence safely to organizational data, processes, permissions, actions, systems, and people.

### Systems thinking is the durable skill.

Platforms, programming languages, vendors, development paradigms, and AI models will continue to change.

Understanding the relationships between **people, processes, information, technology, behavior, and constraints** remains durable.

# Industry Blueprints

A major centerpiece of the professional platform will be an open body of knowledge tentatively referred to as **Industry Blueprints**.

The project will carry forward the architectural philosophy of modular, composable industry solutions while becoming independently developed and vendor-neutral.

Its purpose is to answer:

> **How should important business systems actually work?**

Each blueprint should combine business knowledge with architecture and executable software.

## Blueprint Structure

Each Industry Blueprint can eventually contain four major layers.

### 1. Business Blueprint

Describe the domain independently of technology.

This may include:

* Business problem
* Personas and actors
* Business capabilities
* Terminology
* Processes
* Workflows
* Business rules
* Lifecycle models
* Common requirements
* Common variations
* Regulatory or policy considerations
* Maturity models

### 2. Information & Architecture Blueprint

Describe how the system should be structured.

This may include:

* Domain model
* Entities and relationships
* Modular capabilities
* Bounded contexts
* Business events
* Security concepts
* Integration patterns
* APIs
* State models
* Extensibility approaches
* Audit requirements
* AI interaction points

### 3. Executable Reference Implementation

Provide a working open-source application demonstrating the blueprint.

The objective is not necessarily to create production software that organizations deploy unchanged.

The application makes the architecture **concrete, explorable, and testable**.

People should be able to run the system, inspect its data model, interact with APIs, explore workflows, review the code, and understand how the architectural concepts work in practice.

Reference implementations should favor understandable, mainstream technologies and avoid unnecessary dependencies on commercial enterprise platforms.

Ideally they should:

* Run locally
* Support containerized deployment
* Include high-quality sample data
* Expose documented APIs
* Make the data model visible
* Include tests where appropriate
* Demonstrate integration patterns
* Provide an abstraction for AI/model providers
* Be easy to demonstrate publicly

### 4. Platform Mappings

Where useful, document how the architecture could map onto different implementation platforms.

Examples may include:

* Custom application development
* Microsoft Dynamics 365 / Power Platform
* Salesforce
* ServiceNow
* Cloud-native approaches
* AI-native development platforms
* Hybrid architectures

The blueprint remains independent of those implementations.

# Consulting & Advisory Practice

The public platform supports an independent consulting business.

The consulting business should primarily monetize **judgment, application, and experience**, rather than access to information.

Open knowledge should remain broadly useful and accessible.

Customers pay for help applying that knowledge to their organizations.

Initial service offerings can include:

## Enterprise Application Architecture Review

Independent assessment of an important application or application portfolio, including architecture, data, security, integration, maintainability, governance, and technical risk.

## Application Modernization Assessment

Determine which existing systems should be retained, replaced, consolidated, rebuilt, retired, or rearchitected.

Evaluate the appropriate combination of packaged software, configurable platforms, custom development, open source, and AI-enabled approaches.

## AI & Agentic Systems Readiness Assessment

Evaluate where AI can create meaningful value within enterprise processes and applications and what architectural, data, governance, security, and organizational foundations are required.

## Troubled Project / System Review

Provide an independent assessment when an application program is late, expensive, fragile, poorly adopted, over-customized, difficult to maintain, or architecturally unclear.

## Platform-Specific Assessments

Offer specialized reviews where deep expertise exists, including:

* Dynamics 365
* Power Platform
* Dataverse
* Microsoft business applications
* Application lifecycle management
* Governance
* Modernization

These remain specialized services underneath a vendor-independent advisory practice.

# Implementation Strategy: The Spiral Model

The platform should be built iteratively.

Rather than completing one major component before beginning the others, create a small working version of the entire ecosystem and repeatedly deepen it.

Each spiral should leave behind permanent assets.

Examples include:

* A published page
* A diagram
* An article
* A framework
* A GitHub commit
* A working feature
* A demo
* A video
* A professional contact
* A reusable consulting artifact

The standard for progress is not hours worked.

It is:

> **What useful asset exists today that did not exist yesterday?**

# Operating Principles

### Build assets, not activity.

Favor work that remains useful after the day is over.

### Publish before perfect.

Early versions should be explicitly versioned and allowed to improve publicly.

### Build the whole system shallowly before building one piece deeply.

A small functioning ecosystem creates learning faster than an enormous unfinished project.

### Keep the brand vendor-independent.

Microsoft expertise is valuable evidence of depth, but not the definition of the platform.

### Let real use cases create abstractions.

Avoid designing a universal enterprise architecture before enough examples exist.

### Keep reference code understandable.

The purpose of the code is to make architectural ideas executable and concrete.

### Open the knowledge; charge for judgment.

Frameworks, examples, reference architectures, and code can create trust and industry value.

Consulting revenue comes from applying experience to complex customer situations.

### One piece of work should feed many channels.

Avoid separate editorial calendars for the website, GitHub, YouTube, and LinkedIn.

### Protect independence.

Future employment should be carefully separated from independent intellectual property, client information, confidential employer information, and employer-owned work.

### Follow curiosity.

Technical depth, architecture, AI experimentation, public-sector problems, software engineering, and creative building should all have room within the broader professional identity.

demonstrating decades of experience through current, tangible work.

using articles, diagrams, videos, talks, and professional conversations to teach and share ideas.

consisting of frameworks, models, methodologies, blueprints, and accumulated professional judgment.

> **how serious business systems should be designed, built, modernized, and evolved in the AI era.**

The platform can start very small.

Each spiral makes it deeper.

Each published asset makes the next asset easier to create.
