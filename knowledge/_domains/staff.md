Personnel is usually the largest line in a public budget, and the hardest constraint on
everything the organization wants to do. It is also the domain where the gap between what
managers want and what the rules permit is widest.

## What makes this domain distinct in government

**Merit systems constrain hiring by design.** Civil service exists to prevent patronage, and
the resulting process — announcement, structured assessment, certified eligible lists, veterans'
preference — is slower than commercial hiring on purpose. Blueprints that propose to "speed up
hiring" without engaging with why the delay exists are not usable.

**Pay is set outside the hiring manager's control**, usually by a classification structure and
sometimes by a bargaining agreement. Public employers therefore compete on mission, stability,
and working conditions, which makes the *employee experience* a recruiting lever rather than a
nicety.

**Due process is real.** Public employees frequently hold property interests in their positions.
Performance management and discipline carry procedural requirements that must be designed in.

**Retirement demographics are a structural risk.** A workforce with a large near-retirement
cohort loses institutional knowledge in a way no system captures — which is why knowledge
transfer is listed as a capability rather than an activity.

## Level variance

- **Federal.** A large, uniform, and highly codified system with a specialist HR workforce and
  government-wide hiring platforms.
- **State.** State civil service systems with substantial variation, and often the employer of
  record for staff who deliver locally administered programs.
- **County / municipal.** Merit rules apply but the HR function may be a single generalist.
  Bargaining is often more locally consequential, and competition with neighbouring
  jurisdictions for the same small labor pool is a daily reality.

## Where this domain connects

Workforce capacity is the constraint that most often decides whether a capability improvement
is achievable at all. It is also where AI integration is most politically sensitive: anything
touching hiring assessment, performance evaluation, or discipline carries both legal exposure
and a bargaining obligation.
