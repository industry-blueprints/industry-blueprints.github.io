Also: Purchasing Director, Procurement Manager, Contracting Officer at federal level where the
role carries a personal warrant.

## Role summary

Owns the process by which the organization commits public money to outside parties: policy,
thresholds, competition, evaluation integrity, award, and the contract that follows. The output
is a defensible decision as much as a purchase.

## Goals

- Awards that survive a protest and an audit
- Genuine competition, including from small and local suppliers
- Cycle times that do not cause departments to route around the process
- Contracts that are actually managed after award
- No appearance of favouritism, ever

## Decisions made

- Which procurement method applies, given value, urgency, and what is being bought
- Whether a sole-source justification is adequate — a decision with real personal exposure
- Whether a specification is unduly restrictive of competition
- How evaluation criteria are weighted, which quietly determines the outcome
- Whether to use a cooperative vehicle instead of running a competition

## Pain points

- **Blamed for delay caused by the requirement.** Departments experience competition as
  bureaucracy. Explaining that fairness is the product is a daily conversation.
- **Requirements written as solutions.** A specification naming a product, arriving with a
  deadline, from a department that has already been demonstrated to.
- **No pre-market engagement.** Constrained in talking to suppliers before solicitation, so
  requirements are written without understanding what the market can do.
- **Contract administration is nobody's job.** Post-award ownership sits with the department,
  which has no capacity, so renewals lapse and performance goes unmanaged.
- **Emergency procurement as a habit.** Poor planning arriving as urgency, which erodes the
  competitive process one exception at a time.
- **No portfolio view.** Contracts, grants, licences, and permits in four systems, so the
  question "what is our total exposure to this vendor" has no answer —
  see [Agreement](/data-entities/agreement/).

## What this persona needs from a blueprint

Requirements framed as outcomes and capabilities rather than products — which is exactly what a
vendor-neutral reference architecture is. Evaluation criteria that can be defended. And an
honest account of what a category of technology actually involves, so the specification is
written by someone who understands it.

## Typical questions

- "What problem are we buying a solution to?"
- "Can more than one supplier meet this specification?"
- "Who is administering this contract for the next five years?"
- "Is this genuinely urgent, or late?"
