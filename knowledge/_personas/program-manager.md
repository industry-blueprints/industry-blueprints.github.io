## Role summary

Owns a defined program — a benefit, a service, a grant-funded initiative, a regulatory regime —
including its staff, its budget, its compliance obligations, and its reporting. The layer where
policy meets delivery, and where the gap between the two becomes somebody's problem.

## Goals

- Deliver the program as designed, at volume, within budget
- Stay compliant with the funding conditions, which are frequently more demanding than the program itself
- Keep the reporting accurate and on time
- Retain enough experienced staff to absorb the next policy change
- Have evidence the program is working, before someone asks

## Decisions made

- How policy is operationalized where the guidance is silent — which is often, and which becomes
  de facto policy without ever being written down
- Which exceptions are granted and on what basis
- How caseload is distributed and prioritized
- What gets reported upward, and how a shortfall is characterized
- Whether a process change is worth the disruption of retraining

## Pain points

- **Rules written without knowledge of the caseload.** Eligibility criteria that are unambiguous
  on paper and ambiguous in half of real cases.
- **Reporting burden scales with funding sources.** Three funders, three schedules, three
  definitions of the same count, assembled by hand.
- **Systems that encode the previous policy.** A change takes effect on a date the system cannot
  support, so staff run a manual workaround that becomes permanent.
- **Accountable for outcomes driven by things they do not control** — labor market, housing,
  another department's waiting list.
- **Every improvement competes with running the program.** There is no slack; capacity for change
  has to be taken from delivery.
- **Institutional knowledge in a few heads.** The person who knows why the exception rule exists
  is retiring.

## What this persona needs from a blueprint

Process models that describe the real path including exceptions, not the happy path. Honest
description of current state, because a blueprint that does not recognize their reality has no
credibility. And staged improvements small enough to absorb without stopping delivery.

## Typical questions

- "What do we do when the applicant doesn't fit any of the categories?"
- "Who else has interpreted this rule, and how?"
- "Can I report the same number to all three funders?"
- "How much of my team's time does this take during the transition?"
