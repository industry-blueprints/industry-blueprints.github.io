## Role summary

Owns workforce planning, classification, recruitment, onboarding, learning, performance,
compensation administration, labor relations, and separation. In a merit system, also the
custodian of the process that keeps hiring lawful.

## Goals

- Fill vacancies before service degrades
- Keep hiring defensible under merit and veterans' preference rules
- Reduce attrition, which is the largest avoidable cost in the organization
- Reach agreements that are affordable and implementable
- Have a workforce plan that survives the retirement wave

## Decisions made

- How a position is classified, which sets what it can be paid and who can apply
- Which recruitment method and assessment applies
- Whether a candidate is qualified against published criteria
- What is conceded in bargaining, and what the operational consequence of conceding it is
- Whether a discipline case is supportable under due process

## Pain points

- **Time-to-hire measured in months** while the private sector measures it in weeks. Candidates
  accept other offers mid-process, and the process cannot be shortened by wanting it to be.
- **Pay set by a classification structure** that predates the roles being recruited. Hiring a
  data engineer against a specification written for a systems analyst in 2004.
- **Cannot compete on money.** Competes on mission, stability, and working conditions — which
  means employee experience is a recruiting lever, and it is usually the one being degraded.
- **Retirement cliff with no knowledge transfer.** Decades of undocumented institutional
  understanding leaving on a known schedule.
- **Position control versus reality.** Vacancies held for budget savings are still counted as
  capacity in every plan.
- **Anything touching assessment is high-risk.** Automated screening carries adverse-impact
  exposure, merit-system challenge, and a bargaining obligation simultaneously.

## What this persona needs from a blueprint

Position modelled separately from employee, so vacancy and classification are first-class — see
the [core data model](/data-models/core-public-sector-model/). Realistic capacity assumptions in
any transformation plan, because "the team will absorb it" is where most plans quietly fail. And
clear-eyed treatment of AI in hiring: the legal and labor exposure is high enough that it should
not be recommended casually.

## Typical questions

- "Is this classification going to attract anyone at that pay?"
- "Do we have to bargain this change before we implement it?"
- "What is our time-to-hire, and where in the process is it going?"
- "Who else can do this job when they retire?"
