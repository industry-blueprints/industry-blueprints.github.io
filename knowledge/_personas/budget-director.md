Also: Chief Financial Officer, Finance Director, Comptroller in smaller jurisdictions where the
roles combine.

## Role summary

Runs the annual cycle — call for requests, review, executive recommendation, legislative or
council adoption — then monitors execution against appropriation for the rest of the year.
Simultaneously the organization's most informed generalist about what everything costs and its
least informed specialist about what anything does.

## Goals

- A balanced, adopted budget on the statutory timetable
- No overspend against appropriation, and no material underspend either
- Reserves maintained at policy level
- A clean audit
- Enough visibility to see a problem before it becomes a supplemental request

## Decisions made

- What the departmental request gets cut to, usually with incomplete information about consequence
- Which position vacancies are held to generate savings — the most common and least visible lever
- Whether a mid-year request is funded, deferred, or absorbed
- How one-time versus ongoing money is classified, which determines what can be committed to
- What the fund structure looks like, which quietly constrains everything downstream

## Pain points

- **Requests arrive as narrative, decisions have to be numeric.** Departments describe need;
  the budget office needs a comparable basis and rarely gets one.
- **No outcome information.** Can see what a program costs and how many units it produced.
  Cannot see whether it worked, so cuts fall on what is politically easiest.
- **Position vacancy savings are a fiction that compounds.** Holding posts vacant balances the
  budget and degrades service in a way that surfaces two years later as a crisis.
- **Grant-funded programs with a cliff.** External funding ends; the service and the staff do not.
- **Spreadsheet gravity.** The system of record for the actual working budget is very often a
  spreadsheet, because the financial system models the adopted budget, not the negotiation.
- **Technology requests with no operating cost line.** Capital gets approved; the recurring cost
  arrives later as a surprise.

## What this persona needs from a blueprint

Cost structure they can compare across options, including the operating tail. Outcome measures
that make a program's value arguable rather than assertable. And an honest statement of what a
capability improvement actually requires — staff time is the cost that gets omitted from every
business case and is usually the binding one.

## Typical questions

- "Is this ongoing or one-time?"
- "What is the fully loaded cost in year three?"
- "If we fund this, what does it displace?"
- "What happens to the staff when the grant ends?"
