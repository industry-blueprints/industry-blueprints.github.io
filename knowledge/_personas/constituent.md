Resident, taxpayer, applicant, claimant, licensee, complainant, customer. The label changes with
the transaction; the person does not.

**This persona is first-class and listed before the internal roles it interacts with.** A
blueprint that models only the staff side will optimize the wrong things — faster handling of a
process that should not exist, or a digital channel that works beautifully for the people who
were already fine.

## Who this actually is

Not a single person, and designing for the median is how services fail the people who need them
most. Real variation that changes the design:

- **Confident and connected.** Will self-serve if self-service works. Cheapest to serve. Usually
  the only one represented in user testing.
- **Limited English proficiency.** Needs the service in another language, at every step —
  including the letter that arrives three weeks later.
- **Using assistive technology.** Blind, low-vision, motor or cognitive disability. Statutory
  right of access, routinely broken by a redesign nobody tested.
- **Low or no digital access.** No broadband, phone-only, or shares a device. A "digital by
  default" service excludes them by construction.
- **In crisis.** Recently unemployed, bereaved, evicted, or after a disaster. Reduced capacity to
  navigate complexity, at the exact moment complexity peaks.
- **Acting for someone else.** A parent, an adult child, an advocate, a landlord. Needs
  authorization that most systems model badly or not at all.

## Goals

- Solve the problem and get on with their life
- Know what will happen and when
- Not have to explain the situation more than once
- Not be penalized for a mistake in a form they did not understand
- Be treated as though their time has value

## Pain points

- **Having to know the org chart.** Asked which department they need, when knowing that is the
  organization's job.
- **Repeating themselves.** Every channel, every transfer, every stage.
- **Silence.** No acknowledgement, no status, no idea whether anything is happening. Silence is
  what generates the follow-up contact that everyone counts as demand.
- **Language they cannot act on.** Technically accurate, legally reviewed, incomprehensible.
- **Deadlines they were not told about**, in a notice sent to an old address.
- **No way to correct an error** without starting over.
- **Being asked for something government already has.**

## What good looks like from here

One place to go. Recognition across channels. Plain language in their language. A clear statement
of what happens next and by when. Proactive contact when something changes. And a route to a
person that is genuinely available — including when the automated channel is the one that failed
them.

## Typical questions

- "Who do I talk to about this?"
- "What is happening with my application?"
- "What does this letter mean, and what do I have to do?"
- "Why do you need this again? I sent it last month."
- "Is there someone I can actually speak to?"
