Council member, county commissioner, state legislator, mayor, member of Congress. Not staff, not
a customer, and consequential to nearly every capability in this model.

## Role summary

Approves the budget, sets policy, provides oversight, and represents constituents. At local level
frequently part-time with little or no personal staff, deciding on matters far outside their
professional background, in public, on the record.

## Goals

- Deliver on what they were elected to do
- Resolve the constituent problems that reach them, which is much of the actual job
- Approve budgets they can defend to the people who pay them
- Avoid being surprised — by a failure, a cost overrun, or a story
- Show something concrete before the next election

## Decisions made

- Whether an initiative is funded, and at what level
- Whether policy is adopted, amended, or sent back
- Whether to escalate a constituent complaint publicly
- Where to spend limited political attention
- Whether to trust an administrative recommendation they cannot independently evaluate

## Pain points

- **Asked to decide on things far outside their expertise**, briefed by people with an interest
  in the outcome, in a public session where asking a basic question is a risk.
- **Constituent casework with no visibility.** They pass a complaint to the department and hear
  nothing. When the constituent follows up, they have no answer — an entirely solvable problem
  that damages trust in both directions.
- **The horizon problem.** Multi-year programs deliver after their term. Ribbon-cuttings beat
  deferred maintenance, and everyone involved knows it.
- **Aggregate reporting they cannot use.** Told the department closed 4,200 cases. Cannot tell
  whether their ward is being served.
- **Technology proposals as capital asks.** Presented with a purchase price and no operating tail.
- **Finding out from a resident.** The most reliable way to lose confidence in an administration.

## What this persona needs from a blueprint

Plain-language explanation of what a capability is and why it matters, without jargon or vendor
framing. Staged plans with visible value at each stage, because that is what survives an
electoral cycle. Total cost including the operating tail. And, specifically, **constituent
casework with status visibility** — the single highest-value and most consistently overlooked
thing an organization can give this persona.

## Typical questions

- "What happened with the resident I referred three weeks ago?"
- "What are we actually getting for this?"
- "How is this affecting my district specifically?"
- "What is the ongoing cost, not the purchase price?"
- "Why am I hearing about this now?"
