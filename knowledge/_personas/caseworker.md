Also: Eligibility Specialist, Claims Examiner, Investigator, Social Worker, Compliance Officer,
Adjudicator. Titles differ enormously; the shape of the work does not.

## Role summary

Holds a caseload. For each case: gathers information, verifies it, applies criteria, decides or
recommends, communicates the outcome, and documents enough that the decision can be defended
later. Distinct from a service representative in one important way — **this person's decisions
determine what someone receives or is permitted to do.**

## Goals

- Get the decision right, because getting it wrong affects someone materially
- Keep the caseload from growing
- Not miss a statutory deadline
- Document defensibly, without documentation consuming the day
- Avoid the case that turns into a complaint, an appeal, or a news story

## Decisions made

- Whether the evidence is sufficient, or more is needed — the judgement that drives everything else
- How criteria apply to circumstances the criteria did not anticipate
- Whether to grant, deny, or refer
- Whether a situation warrants escalation or a supervisor's exception
- What to write down, and how much of the reasoning to expose

## Pain points

- **Caseload assumes an average that does not exist.** Standards are set on a typical case;
  real caseloads cluster complex ones, and the complex ones absorb the time.
- **Verification depends on people who are hard to reach.** Employers, landlords, other agencies.
  Waiting is most of the elapsed time and none of it counts as progress.
- **Documentation for the appeal, not for the work.** Written defensively, which takes longer
  and produces records that are hard for the next person to use.
- **Policy changes mid-caseload**, sometimes retroactively, and the system does not support both
  versions.
- **Emotional load with no authority to fix the cause.** Telling someone they do not qualify,
  repeatedly, when the criteria are the problem.
- **Error rates measured, workload drivers not.** Held to accuracy standards while the conditions
  that cause errors go unmeasured.

## What this persona needs from a blueprint

Decision support that shows the criteria and the evidence together rather than requiring both to
be held in the head. Documentation that is a byproduct of the work rather than a task after it.
And any AI assistance to be **advisory and visible** — see
[AI disclosure and human review](/governance/ai-disclosure-and-human-review/). A recommendation
they cannot interrogate is a liability, because the decision remains theirs.

## Typical questions

- "Has anyone decided a case like this before?"
- "What exactly do I need to verify this, and what counts as sufficient?"
- "Which version of the rule applies to a case opened in March?"
- "If I'm wrong, what happens to this person?"
