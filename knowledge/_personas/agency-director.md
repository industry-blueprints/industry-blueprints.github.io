Also: Agency Head, Commissioner, Secretary, Public Works Director, Health Officer, Department
Chief. The person who owns a mission and everything required to deliver it.

## Role summary

Translates political direction and statutory mandate into an operating plan, defends the budget
that funds it, and answers for the result — to an elected principal, an oversight body, the
press, and the public, usually in that order of immediacy.

## Goals

- Deliver the statutory mission without a failure that becomes a headline
- Protect the budget through the appropriation cycle
- Keep enough staff to operate, in a market they cannot outbid
- Show progress on the current administration's priorities without abandoning the standing mandate
- Leave the department more capable than they found it, on a horizon longer than their tenure

## Decisions made

- What the department will and will not do when the mandate exceeds the funding — the defining decision of the role
- Which risks are accepted rather than mitigated
- Where to spend scarce discretionary money
- Whether to escalate a problem upward, which costs political capital either way
- Which improvement initiatives survive a change in leadership

## Pain points

- **Mandate without funding.** New obligations arrive by statute, regulation, or grant condition
  with no accompanying resource, and refusal is not available.
- **Time horizon mismatch.** Transformation takes longer than the administration that commissioned it.
- **Reporting that describes activity.** Asked in a hearing whether the program is working, they
  have outputs — cases closed, permits issued — and no defensible outcome measure.
- **Inheriting technical debt they cannot see.** The system limitation that blocks a policy
  commitment surfaces after the commitment is public.
- **Everything is visible.** Deliberation is often required to be in public, and any misstep is
  a records request away from being quoted.

## What this persona needs from a blueprint

Not architecture. A defensible answer to "what does good look like, what will it cost, and what
happens if we don't." Staged plans where each stage has standalone value, because the plan has to
survive a change of leadership. And measures they can take into a budget hearing that describe
whether the thing worked — see the [measurement framework](/meta/measurement-framework/).

## Typical questions

- "If I have to give back eight percent, what breaks?"
- "What is the smallest version of this that is still worth doing?"
- "Who else has done this, and what did it actually cost them?"
- "If this goes wrong, how does it go wrong?"
