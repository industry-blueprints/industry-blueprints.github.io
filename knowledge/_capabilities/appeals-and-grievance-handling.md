## Purpose

To provide an accessible route for someone to challenge a decision that went against them, review
it independently, and either uphold it with reasons or put it right.

**Like [protest and dispute resolution](/capabilities/protest-and-dispute-resolution/), this
capability produces nothing on its own.** Every appeal is decided on the quality of the original
decision and the record made at the time. What happens here is discovery of how good that was.

## Desired outcomes

- Appeal rights stated in the adverse decision, plainly, with the deadline
- A route that does not require representation or a filing fee
- Review by someone not involved in the original decision
- The record available to the appellant, before they have to argue
- Decisions within a defined period, with reasons
- Interim relief where the consequence of waiting is severe
- Overturn patterns analysed and fed back to the deciding function

## Key processes

Appeal rights notification · appeal intake across channels · standing and timeliness assessment ·
record assembly and disclosure to the appellant · interim relief determination · independent review
· hearing, where one is provided · decision and reasoning · implementation of the outcome ·
escalation to an external forum · pattern analysis and feedback · informal grievance and
complaint handling

## Key data

Appeal register with grounds, timeline, and outcome · the original decision record and evidence
relied on · disclosure log — what was provided to the appellant and when · interim relief
determinations · hearing records · decisions with reasoning · implementation confirmation ·
grounds and outcomes coded for trend analysis · complaint register distinct from formal appeals

## Measures

| Measure | Class |
|---|---|
| Adverse decisions stating appeal rights and the deadline | Process |
| Appeal rate as a share of adverse decisions | Outcome |
| Share overturned, by original deciding unit | Outcome |
| Time from appeal to decision | Process |
| Appellants who obtained the record before arguing | Process |
| Appeals lodged without representation, and their success rate | Outcome |
| Overturn grounds recurring across cases | Outcome |
| Outcomes implemented within the required period | Process |

**Read appeal rate in both directions.** A high rate signals decision quality problems upstream. A
very low rate more often means the route is inaccessible than that decisions are right — and the
unrepresented success rate is the measure that distinguishes them.

## Level variance

- **Federal.** Administrative appeal within the agency, then to administrative law judges, appeals
  councils, and the courts — a long, formal chain with published procedure.
- **State.** Fair hearing systems for benefit programmes with federally mandated timelines and
  continuation of benefits pending appeal, plus licensing board appeals and personnel boards.
- **County / municipal.** Local boards of appeal, hearing officers, and in many cases the elected
  governing body sitting in a quasi-judicial role — which mixes political and adjudicatory
  functions in one forum and is the structural weakness at this level.

## Getting this right

**State appeal rights plainly, up front.** Present in the notice, in small print, in the last
paragraph, phrased statutorily, they are technically given and practically not.

**Give the appellant the record before the hearing.** Arguing against a decision whose basis they
have not seen turns the hearing into a formality.

**Route review away from the original decision-maker's chain.** A supervisor reviewing their own
team's decision is common, understandable in a small organization, and structurally
insufficient — the same problem as
[protests decided by the awarding official](/capabilities/protest-and-dispute-resolution/).

**Set deadlines long enough to understand the decision.** Ten days to appeal a notice written in
terms the recipient cannot parse is not a real opportunity to appeal.

**Provide interim relief where waiting causes real harm.** A benefit terminated, a licence
suspended, and a hearing in eleven weeks means the outcome arrives after the harm is done.

**Analyse overturn patterns and feed them back.** Handling each appeal as an isolated case lets a
systematically wrong interpretation get corrected one appellant at a time while it continues for
everyone who did not appeal.

**Keep complaints and appeals separate.** A complaint about how someone was treated and an appeal
against a decision need different handling; merging them means one of the two is done badly.

**Implement outcomes, not just decisions.** An appeal upheld with the underlying record never
corrected means the same decision gets made again at the next review.
