## Purpose

To prevent occupational injury and illness, manage the consequences when they occur, accommodate
disability, and support the psychological health of a workforce whose exposure is unusual.

**Government employs people whose job is to attend emergencies.** Firefighters, paramedics, police,
child protection workers, emergency dispatchers, and public health staff carry cumulative
psychological exposure as a routine feature of the role, not as an incident. Presumptive injury
statutes in many jurisdictions recognize this, and the operational response frequently has not
caught up.

## Desired outcomes

- Hazards identified and controlled before injury, in the workplaces that carry real ones
- Injuries reported immediately, treated promptly, and analysed for cause
- Return to work supported with genuine modified duty rather than nominal availability
- Accommodation handled as an interactive process, promptly and consistently
- Psychological support that people will actually use
- Health information handled to a higher standard than other employee data

## Key processes

Hazard identification and risk assessment · safety training and certification · personal protective
equipment provision · incident and near-miss reporting · injury intake and medical management ·
workers' compensation claim administration · modified and return-to-work programme · accommodation
request and interactive process · fitness-for-duty evaluation · exposure and surveillance
monitoring · critical incident stress support · employee assistance provision · regulatory
inspection response

## Key data

Hazard register by work location and task · safety training and certification records · incident
and near-miss reports with cause coding · injury and claim records with cost and duration ·
modified duty assignments and outcomes · accommodation requests, determinations, and review dates ·
exposure and surveillance monitoring records · **health information held under access restriction
separate from the personnel file**

| Repeat injuries from a previously identified hazard | Outcome |
| Support programme utilization in high-exposure roles | Output |

**Near-miss reporting is the leading indicator.** A rise usually means reporting is trusted, not
that the workplace became more dangerous — and organizations that read it the other way suppress
the signal they most need.

## Level variance

- **Federal.** Federal employee occupational safety programmes and a distinct federal workers'
  compensation system administered centrally.
- **State.** State occupational safety plans, state workers' compensation systems, and frequently a
  self-insured fund covering agencies and sometimes local participants.
- **County / municipal.** The highest-hazard workforce in government relative to its size — fire,
  police, EMS, public works, water treatment, sanitation, parks. Presumptive coverage for certain
  cancers, cardiac conditions, and post-traumatic stress in public safety roles is expanding and
  materially changes claim exposure. Frequently no dedicated occupational health function, with the
  duty split between HR and [risk
  management](/capabilities/risk-management-and-insurance/).

## What to get right

**Control hazards, not just document them.** Training records complete and toolbox talks logged
satisfy an inspection but prevent nothing unless the underlying hazard is actually controlled.

**Report injuries immediately.** Prompt reporting improves the medical outcome and the claim cost
alike; delay usually reflects a culture where reporting has a consequence, which is worth fixing
directly.

**Make modified duty genuine, not nominal.** A return-to-work programme needs real light duty
available — otherwise the only options are full duty or continued absence.

**Run accommodation as a real interactive process.** A prompt, substantive determination keeps a
request from turning into a complaint.

**Restrict health data to its own access tier, apart from the personnel file.** Keeping medical
information away from supervisors closes a legal exposure and keeps people willing to disclose.

**Code the cause, not just the cost.** Tracking claims by cause as well as cost and department
prevents the same hazard from producing injuries indefinitely — the same discipline described in
[risk management](/capabilities/risk-management-and-insurance/).

**Make psychological support available without going through a supervisor.** Support that requires
self-identification to a supervisor won't be used in a public safety culture, and low utilization
will then be misread as low need.
