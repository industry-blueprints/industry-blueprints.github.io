## Purpose

To take a person from accepted offer to productive contributor — completing pre-employment
requirements, establishing identity and access, issuing equipment, delivering required training,
and integrating them into the work.

**Onboarding is the most cross-functional process in the domain and is usually owned by nobody.**
HR, IT, facilities, security, payroll, and the hiring manager each hold a piece. Each piece works;
the sequence does not.

## Desired outcomes

- Pre-employment requirements completed before the start date, not after
- Accounts, access, and equipment ready on day one
- Access granted by role, at the level the role requires and no more
- Mandatory training completed within its required window
- A named contact for the first weeks who is not the hiring manager alone
- Early attrition understood rather than absorbed

## Key processes

Offer acceptance and pre-boarding · background check, fingerprinting, and clearance · credential
and licence verification · benefits enrolment and payroll setup · identity establishment and
account provisioning · role-based access assignment · equipment and workspace preparation ·
orientation delivery · mandatory and role-specific training · probationary check-ins · early
attrition review

## Key data

New hire record with start date and status of every prerequisite · background and clearance status ·
credential and licence verification with expiry · provisioning checklist by role · role-to-access
mapping · equipment issued and assigned · training completion by requirement · probation milestones
and outcomes

| New hire assessment of the onboarding experience | Outcome |

Ninety-day attrition is the honest outcome measure. A person who leaves that fast usually
encountered something in the first weeks, and the cost of the whole
[recruiting](/capabilities/recruiting-and-hiring/) cycle is lost with them.

## Level variance

- **Federal.** Extended clearance and suitability processing, standardized credentialing, and
  entry-on-duty procedures that can extend the interval from acceptance to start by months.
- **State.** Agency onboarding within statewide HR and payroll systems, with licensure
  verification significant for professional and clinical roles.
- **County / municipal.** Public safety hires with lengthy background investigation, academy
  training, and certification requirements — and, in small jurisdictions, an onboarding process
  that exists as a checklist in one person's head.

## Where it goes wrong

**Provisioning starting on day one.** IT is notified when the person arrives, so the first week is
spent waiting for an account. The commonest and most fixable failure in the whole domain.

**Prerequisites completed after start.** Background check pending while the person is already
working, which is a control failure and occasionally a legal one.

**Access copied from a colleague.** "Give them the same as Dana" is fast and accumulates
entitlements Dana acquired over eight years — the origin of most excess access found in later
reviews. See [identity and access
management](/capabilities/identity-and-access-management/).

**Orientation as a compliance day.** Eight hours of policy delivered in week one, retained by
nobody, with the role-specific knowledge left to informal absorption.

**No named buddy.** The hiring manager is busy, the new person has nobody to ask trivial questions,
and the questions go unasked.

**Probation as a formality.** Confirmed automatically at the end of the period. The probationary
period is the one moment when due-process constraints are lightest, and it is routinely wasted.

**Credential expiry captured once.** Licence verified at hire, expiry recorded nowhere that
watches it, and the lapse discovered during an audit.

**No feedback loop.** Nobody asks the person who just went through it, so the same defects persist
for years.
