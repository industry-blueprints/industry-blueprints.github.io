## Purpose

To organize the people and goods that arrive after a visible event — registering, screening, and
assigning volunteers, and receiving, sorting, storing, and distributing donated material — so that
the surge helps rather than consumes response capacity.

**Spontaneous volunteers and unsolicited goods will arrive.** They arrive within hours, in numbers
nobody requested, and managing them is a workload whether or not the capability exists. An
unplanned response spends response capacity on the people who came to help, and unsolicited goods
frequently become a disposal cost.

## Desired outcomes

- Affiliated volunteers registered, screened, credentialed, and trained in advance
- Spontaneous volunteers received safely and assigned to real needs
- Volunteer skills matched to requirements, with credentials verified where they matter
- Donations directed toward cash, which is what actually helps
- Unsolicited goods managed without consuming response capacity
- Voluntary organizations coordinated rather than duplicated
- Liability, insurance, and workers' compensation coverage settled before an event
- Volunteer hours documented, because they count toward cost share

## Key processes

Affiliated volunteer programme operation — recruitment, screening, credentialing, training ·
volunteer reception centre planning and operation · spontaneous volunteer registration and
just-in-time briefing · skill and credential matching, including professional verification ·
assignment, supervision, and safety · hour tracking and documentation · donations management
planning · cash donation channel establishment and promotion · unsolicited goods receipt, sorting,
storage, and distribution · warehouse and logistics operation · voluntary organization coordination
· unmet needs matching · disposal of unusable goods · recognition and retention

## Key data

Affiliated volunteer register with skills, credentials, screening status, and availability ·
credential verification records for professional roles · reception centre registrations · volunteer
assignments with hours and supervisor · **volunteer hours by category, valued for cost share** ·
donation offers with type, quantity, and location · received goods inventory · distribution records
· voluntary organization directory with capability and capacity · unmet needs register · disposal
records and costs

**Documented volunteer hours have a direct financial value**, counting toward the local cost share
on federal assistance under defined conditions. Undocumented, the hours are still worked and the
jurisdiction pays the share in cash — which makes hour tracking one of the highest-return
administrative tasks in
[recovery](/capabilities/recovery-and-disaster-assistance/).

## Level variance

- **State.** Statewide volunteer registration systems, professional credential verification for
  medical and technical volunteers, and coordination with statewide voluntary organization networks.
- **County.** **Where volunteer reception centres and donations coordination are usually run**,
  alongside the emergency operations centre, with the county coordinating voluntary organizations
  across municipalities.
- **Municipal / tribal.** First point of arrival for spontaneous volunteers and donated goods, and
  where community and faith-based organizations with the deepest local reach actually operate.

## What to get right

**Plan for the surge before it arrives.** Without a plan, the people running the response spend
the first day directing volunteers and goods instead of the response itself.

**Direct unsolicited goods toward cash from the start.** Used clothing, expired food, and unusable
items arriving by the truckload require storage, sorting, and eventually paid disposal — a
promoted cash channel keeps well-intentioned giving from becoming a net cost.

**Establish the cash donation channel early.** The public wants to help immediately; a clear cash
route promoted in the first hours is what keeps the impulse from converting into goods instead.

**Verify credentials before assigning.** Confirming medical and technical volunteers rather than
accepting them on assertion is what closes the liability and occasional safety exposure.

**Track volunteer hours as they're worked.** Documenting hours is what captures the cost share
benefit — see above — instead of losing it.

**Resolve coverage through registration and assignment records.** Creating those records up front
is what settles whether a volunteer injured while working is covered by workers' compensation.

**Coordinate voluntary organizations before the event.** A coordination structure in place ahead
of time is what keeps several organizations from serving the same neighbourhood while another
goes unserved.

**Follow up with registered volunteers.** Contacting people who offered help is what keeps them
willing to offer again next time.
