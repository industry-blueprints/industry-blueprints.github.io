## Purpose

To tell people what services exist, what obligations apply to them, and what is changing — through
channels they actually use, in time for the information to be useful.

**The measure of outreach is not reach; it is whether the people affected acted.** A campaign that
achieves high impressions among people already engaged and misses the households the programme
exists for has failed, and will report as a success.

## Desired outcomes

- Audiences defined by who is affected, not by who is easy to reach
- Channel selection driven by evidence about the audience, not by staff habit
- Messages in plain language and in the languages the community speaks
- Coordination across departments so one household does not receive three versions
- Trusted intermediaries used where direct channels do not reach
- Effect measured as action taken, not as impressions delivered
- Outward messaging consistent with what the contact centre tells people

## Key processes

Audience identification and segmentation · message development and plain-language review ·
translation and cultural adaptation · channel planning and media buying · community and
intermediary partnership · earned media and press relations · social media operation · campaign
delivery and scheduling · targeted notification for obligations and deadlines · effect measurement
· misinformation correction · coordination with [emergency public
warning](/capabilities/public-warning-and-emergency-communication/)

## Key data

Audience segments with size, language, and channel evidence · message inventory with reading level
and translation status · channel performance by segment · partner and intermediary register ·
campaign plans with objective, audience, and success measure · action data linked to campaign
timing — applications, renewals, attendance · complaint and enquiry volume attributable to a
message · press and social records for the public record

## Measures

| Measure | Class |
|---|---|
| Reach within the affected population, by segment | Output |
| Action taken following the message — applications, renewals, compliance | Outcome |
| Take-up change among under-served segments specifically | Outcome |
| Messages available in threshold languages at launch, not later | Process |
| Contact centre volume caused by an unclear message | Outcome |
| Time from decision to public notification | Process |
| Departments issuing conflicting messages on the same subject | Process |

**Take-up among under-served segments is the only measure that distinguishes outreach from
broadcasting.** Aggregate reach reliably improves by talking more to people already reached.

## Level variance

- **Federal.** National campaigns on programme availability and obligations, with statutory
  paperwork and plain-language requirements and formal press operations.
- **State.** Programme outreach for benefits, tax obligations, licensing deadlines, and public
  health — frequently reliant on counties and community organizations for actual delivery.
- **County / municipal.** The most direct relationship and the smallest budget, typically one
  communications officer covering every department. Local media decline has removed the channel
  local government most relied on, and social platforms reach a self-selected audience — which
  makes trusted intermediaries the practical answer at this level.

## Where it goes wrong

**Reach reported as success.** Impressions and followers counted; whether anyone applied, renewed,
or complied is not.

**Channels chosen by staff preference.** A social post and a press release, for an audience that
uses neither.

**Publishing instead of reaching.** Notice posted on the website, obligation deemed communicated,
and the people affected never saw it.

**Translation as the last step.** English campaign launched, translated versions arriving after the
deadline, which is functionally not translating at all — see
[language access](/capabilities/language-access/).

**Deadline messaging arriving too late to act.** The notice is technically in advance and
practically not, given how long the required action takes.

**Contradiction with the front line.** The campaign says one thing, the contact centre says
another, because outward messaging and the internal knowledge base are maintained separately — see
[knowledge management](/capabilities/knowledge-management/).

**Reaching only the organized.** Community meetings and association mailings, which is the same
selection problem described in
[open government and public participation](/capabilities/open-government-and-public-participation/).

**Misinformation left to stand.** Corrected once on one channel while the incorrect version
circulates on three others.
