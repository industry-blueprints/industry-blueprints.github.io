## Purpose

To hold people lawfully and safely — booking and identifying them, classifying and housing them
appropriately, providing health care and basic services, managing movement and court production,
and releasing them correctly and on time.

**A local jail population is mostly people who have not been convicted.** The facility is
frequently the largest provider of mental health and substance use care in its county, holds people
for days on matters that will not result in a custodial sentence, and is the point at which
employment and housing are most often lost.

## Desired outcomes

- Identification established reliably at booking, including for people who cannot cooperate
- Medical and mental health screening at intake, before housing assignment
- Classification driven by assessed risk and vulnerability, not by convenience
- Suicide and self-harm risk identified and acted on in the first hours, when it is highest
- Health care at a community standard, with continuity in and out
- Release timely, correct, and planned — not at midnight with nothing
- Family contact affordable, because contact reduces reoffending
- Population and length of stay actively managed with the court and prosecutor

## Key processes

Booking, identification, and property intake · warrant and hold verification · medical, mental
health, and substance use screening · withdrawal management · suicide risk screening and
observation · classification and housing assignment · vulnerable population protection · programme
and education delivery · medication administration and continuity · sick call and specialty care ·
grievance handling · use of force and restraint review · discipline with due process · visitation,
mail, and communications · court production and transport · release verification and processing ·
release planning — medication, identification, benefits, transport · in-custody death review ·
population and length-of-stay management · detainer and interagency hold coordination

## Key data

Booking records with identity, charges, and holds · intake screening results — medical, mental
health, suicide risk · classification assessments and housing assignments · health records under
restricted access · medication administration records · incident, use of force, and restraint
records · grievances and their disposition · visitation and communications records ·

**Over-detention is the measure with the clearest legal exposure and the simplest cause.** People
held past a court's release order because the disposition did not reach the facility — a
[justice information sharing](/capabilities/justice-information-sharing/) failure that constitutes
unlawful detention.

## Level variance

- **Federal.** Federal prisons and marshals' detention, with local jails frequently housing federal
  detainees under contract — an arrangement that gives small county jails a revenue line and a
  population they did not plan for.
- **State.** State prisons holding sentenced populations, state standards and inspection regimes
  for local jails, and the classification system for transfers.
- **County.** **Operates the jail, almost always under the sheriff.** Holds pretrial detainees,
  people serving short sentences, and state and federal holds. The county pays for detention it
  does not control the inflow to, which is the structural tension in the whole domain.
- **Tribal.** Tribal detention facilities where they exist, with many nations contracting custody to
  county facilities far from the community.

## Where it goes wrong

**Screening skipped or deferred at intake.** The highest-risk period for suicide is the first hours
in custody, and screening delayed until the next shift is screening after the risk.

**Withdrawal management absent.** People arriving in withdrawal from alcohol or opioids, in a
facility with no protocol and no clinician — one of the most common causes of in-custody death and
entirely foreseeable at booking.

**Medication discontinued on entry.** Community prescriptions stopped because verification is slow,
which destabilizes people who were stable and creates a management problem the facility then treats
as behavioural.

**Classification by bed availability.** Vulnerable people housed by convenience, which is how
in-custody assaults happen.

**Isolation used for mental illness.** Segregation as a management tool for people whose behaviour
is a symptom, which worsens the condition reliably.

**Release at midnight with nothing.** No identification, no medication, no transport, no benefits —
and reoffending and overdose risk both peak in the days after release. Release planning is the
cheapest intervention available and the first cut.

**Over-detention from missing dispositions.** Discussed above.

**Family contact priced as a revenue source.** Commission arrangements on calls and video, charged
to families who are usually poor, funding facility operations — while contact is among the
best-evidenced predictors of successful reentry.

**Length of stay unmanaged.** Nobody owning the number, so the population is treated as an input
rather than as something the court, prosecutor, and jail could manage jointly.
