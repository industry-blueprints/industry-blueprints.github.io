## Purpose

To design public-facing services that people can complete successfully — accessible to people with
disabilities, usable by people with low digital confidence, available in the languages the
community speaks, and comprehensible without specialist knowledge.

**Accessibility is a statutory obligation and a design discipline, and treating it as the former
alone produces the audit outcome without the result.** A service can satisfy a conformance
checklist and remain unusable by the people it was meant to serve.

## Desired outcomes

- Services designed around the user's task, not the organization's structure
- Accessibility built in and validated with assistive technology, not retrofitted from an audit
- Plain language throughout, at a reading level the population can meet
- Content available in the languages the community actually speaks
- Digital as the easiest route, never the only one
- Completion measured, and abandonment investigated
- Research conducted with real users, including those the service is hardest for

## Key processes

User research and journey mapping · service definition and pattern selection · content design and
plain-language editing · interaction and interface design · accessibility conformance and
assistive-technology testing · usability testing with disabled and low-confidence users ·
translation and language access · assisted digital and alternative channel provision · analytics
and completion tracking · post-launch iteration · accessibility statement and feedback route

## Key data

Journey maps with pain points and drop-off · content inventory with reading level and last review ·
design pattern library · accessibility conformance results and remediation backlog · usability
findings by participant group · translation inventory and currency · completion and abandonment by
step and by segment · assisted-channel volume

**Contacts caused by the digital service is the measure that ties this capability to a cost
somebody else carries.** A form that generates a phone call has moved work, not removed it — see
[first contact resolution](/kpis/first-contact-resolution/).

## Level variance

- **Federal.** Statutory accessibility requirements for electronic information technology, a legal
  duty to deliver usable digital services and modernize public-facing forms, formal conformance
  reporting, and language access obligations.
- **State.** State accessibility policies aligned to the same technical standard, with language
  access requirements driven by population thresholds.
- **County / municipal.** The most direct service relationship with residents and the least design
  capacity. Most digital services are vendor-supplied, which makes **accessibility a procurement
  requirement** rather than a design one — and the requirement is frequently a checkbox in the
  solicitation validated by nobody. See
  [needs definition](/capabilities/needs-definition-and-acquisition-planning/).

## Where it goes wrong

**Accessibility as an audit at the end.** Findings arrive after launch, remediation competes with
new work, and the backlog persists indefinitely.

**Conformance without testing.** An automated scan passes and the service is unusable with a screen
reader, because automated tools detect a minority of real barriers.

**The form as the service.** The paper form reproduced as a web form, preserving every question
asked for the organization's convenience — the digitization that defines maturity level 2.

**Plain language treated as dumbing down.** Statutory terminology reproduced verbatim because it is
precise, in a service used by people who have never encountered it.

**Translation of the words, not the service.** Content translated, the process and the supporting
channels available only in English.

**Digital-only as a saving.** The people who cannot use the digital route are the people who most
need the service, and removing the alternative transfers the cost to them and to the contact
centre.

**Research with the reachable.** Usability testing recruited from staff and volunteers, never with
the population the service is hardest for.

**Content published and abandoned.** No owner, no review date, and answers that were correct three
policy changes ago — the problem
[knowledge management](/capabilities/knowledge-management/) exists to solve, appearing on the
public side.
