## Purpose

To coordinate the multi-agency response to an incident — activating the emergency operations
centre, establishing objectives, maintaining a common operating picture, ordering and tracking
resources, and supporting field command.

**Coordination is not command.** The emergency operations centre does not run the incident; it
supports the people who do, and it coordinates across organizations it has no authority over —
neighbouring jurisdictions, utilities, hospitals, school districts, non-profits, and private
operators. The capability is influence and information, not control.

## Desired outcomes

- Activation triggered by criteria rather than by judgment under pressure
- Objectives set for each operational period and communicated
- One common operating picture that every participating organization can see
- Resource requests ordered, tracked, and demobilized deliberately
- Documentation adequate for reimbursement, produced during the event
- Unified command where several agencies share jurisdiction
- The public informed in parallel with the response, not after it

## Key processes

Notification and activation · emergency declaration support · emergency operations centre staffing
by function · incident objective setting and action planning by operational period · situation
awareness collection and common operating picture maintenance · resource ordering, tracking, and
demobilization · mutual aid activation · mission assignment to agencies · unified command
coordination · logistics and staging · documentation and cost capture for reimbursement · liaison
with utilities, hospitals, and private operators · transition to
[recovery](/capabilities/recovery-and-disaster-assistance/) · demobilization and after-action review

## Key data

Activation record with level, trigger, and staffing · incident objectives per operational period ·
incident action plans · situation reports and the common operating picture feed · resource requests
with status, assignment, and demobilization · mission assignments and their completion · cost
capture by category and mission for reimbursement · decision log with time, decision, and
authority · shelter and evacuation status · infrastructure status by system

| Reimbursement claimed against reimbursement obtained | Outcome |

**Contemporaneous cost capture is the measure with the largest financial consequence.** Federal
reimbursement depends on documentation created during the event; reconstructed afterwards, it is
routinely disallowed, and the jurisdiction absorbs costs it was entitled to recover.

## Level variance

- **Federal.** National coordination structures, federal resources and teams provided on request
  through the state, and the reimbursement framework everything downstream is documented for.
- **State.** State emergency operations centre coordinating between counties and requesting federal
  assistance; the required conduit for most federal support.
- **County.** **The primary multi-agency coordination level.** The county operations centre
  coordinates municipalities, special districts, and non-governmental partners, and is where most
  real activations are run.
- **Municipal / tribal.** Field command and local operations centres, with the smallest
  jurisdictions relying on the county for coordination capacity. Tribal governments coordinate
  government-to-government and may request federal assistance directly.

## What to get right

**Capture cost as the event unfolds, not weeks later.** Reconstructing the cost record afterwards
from memory and partial timesheets, once the response has moved on, is the most expensive single
failure in this capability — building the record contemporaneously avoids it.

**Maintain one common operating picture, shared, not reconciled by phone.** Each agency maintaining
its own status and reconciling verbally on conference calls leaves the picture always an hour old
and never truly shared.

**Set activation criteria in advance.** Without criteria, activation is left to a judgment call made
by the same person who is also managing the early response, which makes it late.

**Staff operations centre positions with people trained for them.** Filling positions with whoever
is available means learning the role during the event.

**Track every resource request from order to demobilization.** Untracked, a resource can arrive
unassigned and unaccounted for — and stay on the payroll days after the need ended.

**Keep a decision log.** A record of what was decided, when, and on whose authority matters
enormously in the review that always follows.

**Connect public information directly to the response.** The operations centre needs to feed the
public channel in real time — knowing the road is closed does no good if the public channel does
not know it too. See
[public warning](/capabilities/public-warning-and-emergency-communication/).

**Prompt demobilization as a deliberate decision.** Resources retained past need because releasing
them requires a decision nobody is prompted to make cost money for no benefit.
