## Purpose

To assess responses against the criteria published in the solicitation, reach a decision that
represents best value on the stated basis, and record the reasoning well enough that it can be
explained to an unsuccessful supplier and defended in a challenge.

**The record is the deliverable.** A sound decision with a thin record loses a protest; a
mediocre decision with a complete record usually survives one. That asymmetry shapes how the whole
stage must be run.

## Desired outcomes

- Evaluation strictly against published criteria and weightings, unchanged after opening
- Evaluators independent before consensus, with conflicts declared against the actual bidder list
- Scores supported by written rationale referencing the response
- Departures from the ranked outcome recorded with a stated, criteria-relevant basis
- Debriefs specific enough that a supplier can improve
- Award and its basis published where required

## Key processes

Evaluator panel formation and conflict declaration · evaluation plan and scoring guidance ·
responsiveness and eligibility screening · independent technical scoring · commercial and price
evaluation · consensus and moderation · clarification requests · negotiation or best-and-final
offers · award recommendation and approval · notification and standstill · debrief delivery ·
award publication

## Key data

Evaluation plan with criteria, weightings, and method · conflict declarations against the actual
bidder list · individual scores with written rationale · consensus record and how differences were
resolved · clarification correspondence · price analysis · award recommendation with reasoning ·
debrief records · protest correspondence

A protest rate of zero is not necessarily good. It can mean suppliers have concluded that
challenging is pointless.

## Level variance

- **Federal.** Structured source selection with documented trade-off analysis, statutory debriefing
  rights, and protest forums with the authority to require corrective action.
- **State.** Central procurement rules governing panel composition and scoring method, with an
  administrative protest process and defined standstill periods.
- **County / municipal.** Award frequently requires approval by the governing body in public
  session, which introduces a political stage after the technical one — and is the most common
  point at which the funded outcome departs from the ranked outcome.

## Where it goes wrong

**Criteria adjusted after opening.** Weightings revisited once the responses are visible.
Sometimes innocent, always fatal in a challenge.

**Consensus that overwrites independence.** Scores discussed before individual assessment, so the
panel converges on the loudest evaluator and the independent record does not exist.

**Scores without rationale.** A number with no words attached cannot be defended, explained, or
audited.

**Generic conflict declarations.** Signed annually, in the abstract, rather than against the
actual list of bidders — the specific failure
[competition and evaluation integrity](/governance/competition-and-evaluation-integrity/)
addresses.

**Ranked list departed from silently.** The reasoning may be legitimate; unrecorded, it is
indistinguishable from favouritism. The identical failure appears in
[merit review](/governance/merit-review-integrity/) on the grants side.

**Debriefs that say nothing.** "Your bid was not the most advantageous." The supplier learns
nothing, cannot improve, and stops bidding — which shows up two cycles later as declining
competition.

**Price evaluated separately from what was offered.** Lowest price on a non-comparable scope,
which is a specification failure surfacing at evaluation.
