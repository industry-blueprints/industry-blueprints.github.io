## Purpose

To define what conduct is permitted for people exercising public authority, collect the
disclosures that make conflicts visible, and investigate allegations to a standard that holds.

**The operative test is usually appearance, not corruption.** Most ethics failures in government
involve no personal gain — they involve a relationship that was not disclosed, and a decision that
consequently cannot be defended.

## Desired outcomes

- Disclosure obligations known to those who carry them, before they need to
- Conflicts declared against actual matters rather than in the abstract
- Recusal that is effective, not merely recorded
- Advice available before the decision, and known to be available
- Allegations investigated promptly, impartially, and to a consistent standard
- Reporters protected, and visibly so

## Key processes

Standards issuance and training · financial disclosure filing and review · conflict declaration
against specific matters · recusal management and screening · gift and hospitality registration ·
outside employment and post-employment review · advice requests and opinions · allegation intake
and triage · investigation · determination and sanction · reporter protection

## Key data

Disclosure filings with review status · matter-specific conflict declarations · recusal register
with screens applied · gift register · outside-employment approvals · advice opinions, indexed for
reuse · allegation and investigation case records with restricted access · sanction record

| Recurrence of the same breach type | Outcome |

**Advice requests are the counter-intuitive measure.** More of them means people know the rules
exist and where the line is. An organization with no advice requests does not have no problems.

## Level variance

- **Federal.** A formal ethics programme with designated agency ethics officials, public and
  confidential financial disclosure tiers, criminal conflict-of-interest statutes, and independent
  inspectors general.
- **State.** An ethics commission with jurisdiction over both officials and employees, and
  disclosure regimes for elected and senior appointed roles that differ from those for staff.
- **County / municipal.** Smaller communities where relationships are unavoidable — the contractor
  is a neighbour, the applicant is a former colleague. The answer is disclosure and recusal
  discipline, not pretending the relationships do not exist. Frequently no dedicated ethics
  function, so the duty sits with an attorney or clerk alongside other work.

## Where it goes wrong

**Disclosure collected and not read.** Filed, stored, never reviewed against actual decisions.
The obligation is satisfied and the purpose is not.

**Generic declarations.** "I have no conflicts" signed annually tells you nothing about the
specific award decided in March. This is why
[competition and evaluation integrity](/governance/competition-and-evaluation-integrity/) and
[merit review integrity](/governance/merit-review-integrity/) both require declaration against the
actual list of parties.

**Recusal on paper only.** The person steps out of the vote and remains in the discussion, or
retains system access to the matter. A recusal without a screen is a record, not a control.

**Investigations that drift.** Months without determination damages the subject, the reporter, and
confidence in the process at once. Ethics allegations are investigated as
[investigations](/capabilities/investigations/) — including the employee-rights sequencing, which
applies here in full and is frequently missed because an ethics matter is treated as a personnel
question rather than a potentially criminal one.

**Reporter identity leaking.** Once it happens, reporting stops, and the organization's ability to
detect anything internally goes with it.

**Ethics treated as annual training.** A completion statistic, no advice channel, and no
recollection of the content by the time it matters.

**No post-employment rules.** The staff member who administered a contract joins the supplier, and
nobody had a basis to say anything.
