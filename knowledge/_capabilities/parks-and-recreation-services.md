## Purpose

To provide recreation programmes, operate recreational facilities, and steward parks and open space
— delivering the programming, registration, reservations, and access that make them usable.

**Distinct from the physical asset.** Maintaining the fields, buildings, and trails is
[maintenance management](/capabilities/maintenance-management/) and
[asset management](/capabilities/asset-management/); this capability is the service delivered
through them. Confusing the two is why parks departments are frequently funded as a maintenance
line and measured on acreage rather than on use.

## Desired outcomes

- Park and facility access distributed so that proximity does not depend on income
- Programming matched to community demand rather than to historical schedule
- Registration easy, and pricing that does not exclude
- Fee assistance available without a process that deters applying
- Facilities and programmes accessible to people with disabilities, in practice
- Reservations and permits handled predictably, including for community use
- Youth programming aligned with the hours families actually need
- Usage measured, including by whom

## Key processes

Programme planning and scheduling · instructor and seasonal staff recruitment and certification ·
registration, waitlisting, and payment · fee setting, scholarship, and waiver administration ·
facility and field reservation and permitting · special event permitting · aquatics operation and
lifeguard certification · youth and senior programme delivery · out-of-school-time and summer
programming · sports league administration · park ranger and site operations · volunteer and
friends-group coordination · concession and partnership management · open space and trail
stewardship · park planning and level-of-service analysis · community needs assessment

## Key data

Programme catalogue with capacity, schedule, price, and location · registrations, waitlists, and
attendance · participant demographics and residency where collected · fee assistance applications
and awards · facility and field reservations with utilization · permit applications and outcomes ·
park inventory with amenities, condition, and
[location](/data-entities/location/) · **park access analysis — population within walking distance,
by area** · volunteer hours · revenue by programme against cost · needs assessment findings

**Walkable park access by neighbourhood is the measure that reframes this capability.** Total
acreage is the number usually reported and it says nothing about who can reach a park; the
distribution is a legacy of historical investment patterns and is measurable in an afternoon with
existing [spatial data](/capabilities/geospatial-information-management/).

## Level variance

- **Federal.** National parks, forests, and recreation areas, plus the outdoor recreation grant
  programmes that fund a great deal of state and local acquisition — with obligations attaching to
  the land in perpetuity.
- **State.** State park systems with camping, day use, and reservation systems at scale, plus
  administration of federal recreation funding to local governments.
- **County.** Regional parks, trails, sports complexes, and open space preservation, frequently
  serving municipalities without their own facilities.
- **Municipal.** **The densest and most-used provision** — neighbourhood parks, playgrounds,
  recreation centres, pools, and the youth programming families depend on. Also frequently the
  first department cut in a downturn, and among the last restored.

## Where it goes wrong

**Measured by acreage.** Total park land reported, distribution unexamined, so a jurisdiction with
one large park and no neighbourhood provision reports well.

**Programming set by history.** The same catalogue each season because it is what was run last
season, with waitlists in one category and empty classes in another and no needs assessment
between them.

**Registration that rewards speed and connectivity.** Popular programmes filling in minutes online,
which selects for households with a fast connection and a free morning.

**Fee assistance behind an application nobody sees.** Available, unadvertised, requiring
documentation, and claimed by a fraction of eligible families — the same
[take-up](/capabilities/eligibility-determination/) failure as in benefits.

**Accessibility as a facility question only.** A ramp at the recreation centre and no adaptive
programming, no trained staff, and no inclusion support — so the building is accessible and the
service is not.

**Cost recovery targets applied uniformly.** A recovery rate imposed across all programmes converts
youth and senior programming into a pricing problem, and the participants lost are predictable.

**Reservation systems that favour organized groups.** Leagues holding standing bookings for fields
that sit empty, while informal community use has no route to book.

**Deferred maintenance presented as a parks problem.** Closed pools and unusable fields originating
in the [capital
programme](/capabilities/capital-planning-and-programming/), and attributed to the department that
operates them.

**Seasonal staffing shortages unplanned.** Lifeguard and instructor certification pipelines that
must be built months ahead, addressed in spring, and the pool does not open.
