## Overview

To establish where the organization intends to be in three to five years, what it will do to get
there, and what it will stop doing to make room — expressed specifically enough that a budget
decision can be tested against it.

## Why it matters in the public sector

This capability helps a public agency exercise authority openly, lawfully, and accountably. It gives leaders and staff a shared way to coordinate the work, make decisions visible, and understand whether it is producing the intended result.

## Personas

- Agency or Department Director
- Elected Official
- Program Manager

## Common processes

1. Environmental scan
2. stakeholder and community input
3. objective setting
4. initiative identification and sequencing
5. resource and capacity assessment
6. plan adoption and publication
7. cascade to departmental plans
8. periodic review and re-baselining

## Outcomes and measures

- Direction that survives a change of leadership, or degrades gracefully when it doesn't
- Objectives few enough to be real and specific enough to be falsifiable
- Initiatives staged so each stage delivers standalone value
- Explicit statements of what will not be done
- A line from every funded initiative to an objective, and from every objective to a measure

| Measure | Class |
|---|---|
| Objectives with a named owner and a measure | Process |
| Funded initiatives traceable to an objective | Process |
| Initiatives stopped or deferred deliberately | Process |
| Plan objectives still current after an administration change | Outcome |
| Movement on the outcome measures the plan claimed | Outcome |

The third measure is the honest one. A portfolio in which nothing is ever stopped is a wish list.
