## Overview

To design public-facing services that people can complete successfully — accessible to people with
disabilities, usable by people with low digital confidence, available in the languages the
community speaks, and comprehensible without specialist knowledge.

**Accessibility is a statutory obligation and a design discipline, and treating it as the former
alone produces the audit outcome without the result.** A service can satisfy a conformance
checklist and remain unusable by the people it was meant to serve.

## Why it matters in the public sector

This capability makes information and technology dependable, usable, protected, and available to the work that relies on them. It gives leaders and staff a shared way to coordinate the work, make decisions visible, and understand whether it is producing the intended result.

## Personas

- Constituent
- Solution Architect
- Knowledge Manager

## Common processes

1. User research and journey mapping
2. service definition and pattern selection
3. content design and plain-language editing
4. interaction and interface design
5. accessibility conformance and assistive-technology testing
6. usability testing with disabled and low-confidence users
7. translation and language access
8. assisted digital and alternative channel provision
9. analytics and completion tracking
10. post-launch iteration
11. accessibility statement and feedback route

## Outcomes and measures

- Services designed around the user's task, not the organization's structure
- Accessibility built in and validated with assistive technology, not retrofitted from an audit
- Plain language throughout, at a reading level the population can meet
- Content available in the languages the community actually speaks
- Digital as the easiest route, never the only one
- Completion measured, and abandonment investigated
- Research conducted with real users, including those the service is hardest for

| Measure | Class |
|---|---|
| Task completion rate, unassisted | Outcome |
| Abandonment by step | Process |
| Completion disaggregated by segment — age, language, assistive technology | Outcome |
| Accessibility conformance, and issues found in assistive-technology testing | Process |
| Reading level of published content against target | Process |
| Contacts to the contact centre caused by the digital service | Outcome |
| Content reviewed within its review period | Process |
| Users involved in research who use assistive technology | Process |

**Contacts caused by the digital service is the measure that ties this capability to a cost
somebody else carries.** A form that generates a phone call has moved work, not removed it — see
first contact resolution.
