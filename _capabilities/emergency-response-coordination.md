## Overview

To coordinate the multi-agency response to an incident — activating the emergency operations
centre, establishing objectives, maintaining a common operating picture, ordering and tracking
resources, and supporting field command.

**Coordination is not command.** The emergency operations centre does not run the incident; it
supports the people who do, and it coordinates across organizations it has no authority over —
neighbouring jurisdictions, utilities, hospitals, school districts, non-profits, and private
operators. The capability is influence and information, not control.

## Why it matters in the public sector

This capability helps an agency protect safety, health, continuity, and community wellbeing before, during, and after disruptive events. It gives leaders and staff a shared way to coordinate the work, make decisions visible, and understand whether it is producing the intended result.

## Personas

- Agency or Department Director
- Program Manager
- Elected Official

## Common processes

1. Notification and activation
2. emergency declaration support
3. emergency operations centre staffing by function
4. incident objective setting and action planning by operational period
5. situation awareness collection and common operating picture maintenance
6. resource ordering, tracking, and demobilization
7. mutual aid activation
8. mission assignment to agencies
9. unified command coordination
10. logistics and staging
11. documentation and cost capture for reimbursement
12. liaison with utilities, hospitals, and private operators
13. transition to recovery
14. demobilization and after-action review

## Outcomes and measures

- Activation triggered by criteria rather than by judgment under pressure
- Objectives set for each operational period and communicated
- One common operating picture that every participating organization can see
- Resource requests ordered, tracked, and demobilized deliberately
- Documentation adequate for reimbursement, produced during the event
- Unified command where several agencies share jurisdiction
- The public informed in parallel with the response, not after it

| Measure | Class |
|---|---|

| Positions filled by trained personnel at activation | Process |

| Costs captured contemporaneously versus reconstructed | Process |
| Decisions logged with time and authority | Process |
| Resources demobilized promptly after need ended | Process |
| Reimbursement claimed against reimbursement obtained | Outcome |

**Contemporaneous cost capture is the measure with the largest financial consequence.** Federal
reimbursement depends on documentation created during the event; reconstructed afterwards, it is
routinely disallowed, and the jurisdiction absorbs costs it was entitled to recover.
