## Overview

To provide an accessible route for someone to challenge a decision that went against them, review
it independently, and either uphold it with reasons or put it right.

**Like protest and dispute resolution, this
capability produces nothing on its own.** Every appeal is decided on the quality of the original
decision and the record made at the time. What happens here is discovery of how good that was.

## Why it matters in the public sector

This capability shapes how people and organizations experience public services, requests, and decisions. It gives leaders and staff a shared way to coordinate the work, make decisions visible, and understand whether it is producing the intended result.

## Personas

- Constituent
- Caseworker
- Internal Auditor

## Common processes

1. Appeal rights notification
2. appeal intake across channels
3. standing and timeliness assessment
4. record assembly and disclosure to the appellant
5. interim relief determination
6. independent review
7. hearing, where one is provided
8. decision and reasoning
9. implementation of the outcome
10. escalation to an external forum
11. pattern analysis and feedback
12. informal grievance and complaint handling

## Outcomes and measures

- Appeal rights stated in the adverse decision, plainly, with the deadline
- A route that does not require representation or a filing fee
- Review by someone not involved in the original decision
- The record available to the appellant, before they have to argue
- Decisions within a defined period, with reasons
- Interim relief where the consequence of waiting is severe
- Overturn patterns analysed and fed back to the deciding function

| Measure | Class |
|---|---|
| Adverse decisions stating appeal rights and the deadline | Process |
| Appeal rate as a share of adverse decisions | Outcome |
| Share overturned, by original deciding unit | Outcome |
| Time from appeal to decision | Process |
| Appellants who obtained the record before arguing | Process |
| Appeals lodged without representation, and their success rate | Outcome |
| Overturn grounds recurring across cases | Outcome |
| Outcomes implemented within the required period | Process |

**Read appeal rate in both directions.** A high rate signals decision quality problems upstream. A
very low rate more often means the route is inaccessible than that decisions are right — and the
unrepresented success rate is the measure that distinguishes them.
