## Overview

To hold people lawfully and safely — booking and identifying them, classifying and housing them
appropriately, providing health care and basic services, managing movement and court production,
and releasing them correctly and on time.

**A local jail population is mostly people who have not been convicted.** The facility is
frequently the largest provider of mental health and substance use care in its county, holds people
for days on matters that will not result in a custodial sentence, and is the point at which
employment and housing are most often lost.

## Why it matters in the public sector

This capability supports public safety and justice work where rights, liberty, safety, evidence, and timely decisions matter greatly. It gives leaders and staff a shared way to coordinate the work, make decisions visible, and understand whether it is producing the intended result.

## Common processes

1. Booking, identification, and property intake
2. warrant and hold verification
3. medical, mental health, and substance use screening
4. withdrawal management
5. suicide risk screening and observation
6. classification and housing assignment
7. vulnerable population protection
8. programme and education delivery
9. medication administration and continuity
10. sick call and specialty care
11. grievance handling
12. use of force and restraint review
13. discipline with due process
14. visitation, mail, and communications
15. court production and transport
16. release verification and processing

## Outcomes and measures

- Identification established reliably at booking, including for people who cannot cooperate
- Medical and mental health screening at intake, before housing assignment
- Classification driven by assessed risk and vulnerability, not by convenience
- Suicide and self-harm risk identified and acted on in the first hours, when it is highest
- Health care at a community standard, with continuity in and out
- Release timely, correct, and planned — not at midnight with nothing
- Family contact affordable, because contact reduces reoffending
- Population and length of stay actively managed with the court and prosecutor

**Over-detention is the measure with the clearest legal exposure and the simplest cause.** People
held past a court's release order because the disposition did not reach the facility — a
justice information sharing failure that constitutes
unlawful detention.
