## Overview

To manage government information as a record: classified, retained for its required period,
protected, findable, and disposed of at the end of its life under authority.

## Why it matters in the public sector

This capability makes information and technology dependable, usable, protected, and available to the work that relies on them. It gives leaders and staff a shared way to coordinate the work, make decisions visible, and understand whether it is producing the intended result.

## Personas

- Records Manager
- Privacy Officer
- Internal Auditor

## Common processes

1. Retention schedule development and approval
2. record type identification and classification
3. retention application in systems
4. legal hold placement and release
5. disposition review and authorization
6. destruction certification
7. system decommissioning and content transfer
8. staff training on what constitutes a record

## Outcomes and measures

- Every record type mapped to a retention class
- Retention applied in the systems that hold the records, not on paper only
- Legal holds applied reliably and lifted deliberately
- Disposition that is authorized and documented, not merely deletion
- Responsive material findable within a statutory response period

| Measure | Class |
|---|---|

"Records past retention still held" is the measure most organizations avoid computing, and the
one that best describes actual exposure.
