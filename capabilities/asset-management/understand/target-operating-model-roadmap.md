## What this covers

A target operating model describes how the capability can work as a coherent whole in the future.
It brings together public purpose, services, lifecycle, performance, execution, accountability,
controls, information, stewardship, improvement priorities, partners, and governance.

A roadmap describes how the organization can move from its current position toward that target.
It sequences capability changes, dependencies, learning, adoption, and investment. The target gives
the roadmap direction; the roadmap keeps the target grounded in practical change.

## How it applies to Asset Management

An Asset Management target operating model commonly addresses:

- **Purpose and scope:** portfolios, services, outcomes, mandates, boundaries, and dependencies.
- **Services and lifecycle:** how assets enter, support service, receive care and investment, and leave service.
- **Organization and accountability:** service ownership, asset stewardship, decision rights, specialist roles, and cross-functional forums.
- **Processes and controls:** consistent core practices, proportional variations, decision evidence, approvals, and escalation.
- **Information and stewardship:** shared meaning, durable identity, authoritative responsibilities, quality, access, history, and records.
- **Performance and improvement:** service, asset, process, portfolio, stewardship, and transformation measures.
- **Partners and sourcing:** responsibilities of operators, suppliers, contractors, shared services, regional partners, and capital delivery teams.
- **Workforce and adoption:** skills, capacity, guidance, incentives, communication, and support for new ways of working.
- **Governance:** how priorities, standards, exceptions, performance, architecture, benefits, and improvement are reviewed.

The model does not need to centralize every responsibility. It makes common direction and local
variation explicit so teams can coordinate where shared practice creates value.

### From opportunities to capability increments

Individual improvements are easier to deliver when grouped into useful capability increments. For
example, “trusted portfolio foundation” may combine stewardship, asset identity, classification,
handover, source decisions, reconciliation, training, and integration. A technical implementation
alone would not create the intended operating result.

### Roadmaps represent dependencies and learning

Roadmaps are more credible when they show prerequisites, decision points, pilots, transition
states, adoption, and measures—not only project dates. Some improvements can begin in parallel;
others depend on clearer ownership, information, standards, procurement, or evidence.

## Assessment

Useful questions include:

1. Does the target model describe a complete capability rather than a future system or organization chart?
2. Can its major elements be traced to outcomes, operating evidence, maturity findings, and priorities?
3. Are enterprise, portfolio, program, regional, partner, and local responsibilities distinguishable?
4. Are governance, stewardship, decision rights, measures, workforce, adoption, and continuous improvement explicit?
5. Does the roadmap combine business and technical change into coherent capability increments?
6. Are dependencies, constraints, sequencing, transition states, decision points, and capacity visible?
7. Are near-term actions useful even if longer-term funding or technology choices change?
8. Can leaders review benefits, learning, risk, adoption, and changing priorities over time?

Useful evidence may include all earlier artifacts, organization and governance models, workforce
plans, initiative portfolios, budgets, contracts, product roadmaps, policy timelines, dependency
maps, delivery capacity, benefits evidence, and lessons from prior transformations.

## Maturity progression

| Level | What it looks like | Representative evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Recognized** | Teams hold a shared ambition and pursue improvements through available projects and leadership direction. | Vision statements, local roadmaps, project plans |
| **2 — Described** | A future operating model and roadmap document major changes, ownership, timing, and dependencies. | Target model, initiative roadmap, named owners |
| **3 — Connected** | Business, information, workforce, governance, and technology changes form traceable capability increments. | Integrated roadmap, dependency map, artifact traceability |
| **4 — Governed through outcomes** | Leaders use outcomes, measures, capacity, risk, adoption, and delivery evidence to steer the roadmap. | Portfolio reviews, benefit evidence, decision records |
| **5 — Kept current** | The target and roadmap evolve through learning, service change, completed increments, and emerging needs. | Rolling roadmap, refreshed target, retired assumptions |

The target is a useful direction and change system, not a permanently fixed end state. Longer-lived
asset services benefit from a target that can guide several technology and budget cycles.

## Workshop

### Target operating model and roadmap workshop

| Item | Guidance |
|---|---|
| **Participants** | Executive sponsor, service and asset leaders, operations, maintenance, engineering, finance, capital planning, workforce, performance, governance, data, architecture, product, procurement, change, and delivery leads |
| **Preparation** | Bring all business artifacts, priority decisions, current initiatives, capacity, budgets, contracts, technical direction, and implementation constraints |
| **Typical length** | A half-day to assemble the target, followed by focused roadmap and governance sessions |
| **Result** | An agreed target operating model, capability increments, transition path, ownership, measures, and governance cadence |

Suggested activities:

1. Restate the outcomes, scope, design considerations, and priority improvements.
2. Describe the future capability across services, lifecycle, organization, work, decisions, information, measures, partners, and governance.
3. Identify changes from current practice and group them into coherent capability increments.
4. Map dependencies, constraints, decision points, pilots, adoption needs, and enabling technical work.
5. Sequence near-, medium-, and longer-term change using value, readiness, capacity, and learning.
6. Assign outcome ownership, delivery responsibility, measures, and review forums.
7. Record assumptions and define how evidence can refresh the target and roadmap.

## What you will produce

The **Target Operating Model and Capability Roadmap** completes the Asset Management Capability
Blueprint. At minimum it contains:

- target purpose, scope, outcomes, and design considerations;
- target services, lifecycle, performance, processes, decisions, responsibilities, controls, and information stewardship;
- target organization, partners, workforce, sourcing, measures, and governance;
- prioritized capability increments and their intended outcomes;
- current-to-target changes, dependencies, transition states, decision points, and sequencing;
- adoption, capacity, policy, procurement, funding, and delivery considerations;
- ownership, measures, benefits, review cadence, assumptions, and open decisions;
- traceability to all preceding business artifacts.

Together with the first five outputs, it forms the **Asset Management Capability Blueprint**. The
blueprint is complete enough to guide architecture and implementation when leaders can explain the
target capability, why each change matters, and how progress will be governed and measured.

## Worked example

An illustrative facilities roadmap might use three capability increments:

| Horizon | Capability increment | Business result |
|---|---|---|
| **Near term** | Trusted portfolio foundation | Accountable ownership, shared identity, priority asset coverage, agreed classifications, and improved project handover |
| **Next** | Connected condition and work planning | Consistent inspections, visible condition and work history, service criticality, coordinated maintenance, and portfolio measures |
| **Later** | Integrated renewal and scenario planning | Comparable lifecycle options, funding scenarios, traceable priorities, benefit review, and rolling portfolio decisions |

Workforce, stewardship, governance, information, and technical changes occur within every increment.
The horizons represent dependency and learning rather than a promise that all portfolios advance at
the same pace.
