## What this covers

Improvement begins by describing a worthwhile change in the capability, not by selecting a product.
An opportunity may improve a public service, lifecycle handoff, process, decision, control,
information set, workforce experience, or enabling tool. This stage turns the earlier assessment
and models into a coherent, prioritized portfolio of improvements.

Prioritization makes tradeoffs visible. It considers public and service value, urgency, feasibility,
readiness, dependencies, cost, risk, learning value, and capacity for change. It does not reduce
judgment to a single score; it gives decision-makers a consistent basis for comparing unlike ideas.

## How it applies to Asset Management

Representative improvement themes include:

- strengthen asset identity, hierarchy, ownership, location, and portfolio coverage;
- make project handover complete, timely, and usable by operations and maintenance;
- improve inspection coverage, condition evidence, and consistency across asset classes;
- connect service importance, criticality, condition, work, cost, and risk in planning decisions;
- increase planned care and coordinate maintenance with operations and capital work;
- reduce duplicate capture and manual reconciliation across operational, spatial, and financial records;
- make renewal need, backlog, assumptions, and funding implications easier to explain;
- improve field access, offline work, evidence capture, and feedback to planning;
- provide dependable portfolio measures and scenario analysis;
- build workforce knowledge, stewardship, governance, and adoption alongside technology change.

### Frame opportunities as results

An opportunity statement is more useful when it identifies:

- the service, work, decision, information, or stakeholder experience to improve;
- the evidence that the opportunity is meaningful;
- the expected outcome and how it could be observed;
- the people and portfolios affected;
- enabling business and technical capabilities;
- dependencies, constraints, uncertainty, and implementation considerations.

“Implement an asset platform” names a possible solution. “Give maintenance and capital planners a
shared, current view of asset condition, work history, service importance, and planned investment”
describes the capability improvement that options can be evaluated against.

### AI as one kind of opportunity

AI opportunities are considered alongside other changes. Useful questions are:

- **Where** could assistance, prediction, classification, summarization, search, or detection add value?
- **When** is AI appropriate given data, consequence, explainability, variability, and human expertise?
- **How** would people review, approve, override, monitor, and remain accountable for its use?

Asset Management examples may include organizing inspection evidence, summarizing work history,
finding relevant manuals, forecasting demand or deterioration, identifying unusual patterns, or
supporting investment scenarios. An AI option does not displace the need for dependable information,
clear decisions, professional judgment, and measurable outcomes.

## Assessment

Useful questions include:

1. Are opportunities connected to evidence and a defined service, operating, information, decision, or workforce result?
2. Does the portfolio include nontechnical changes as well as technology opportunities?
3. Are dependencies such as stewardship, process clarity, skills, policy, procurement, data readiness, and integration visible?
4. Are public value, service consequence, urgency, feasibility, cost, risk, and adoption considered consistently?
5. Are related opportunities grouped into coherent capability increments rather than isolated projects?
6. Are assumptions, uncertainty, expected measures, and learning goals explicit?
7. Are AI opportunities evaluated for suitability, human accountability, information readiness, controls, and measurable value?
8. Can leaders explain why selected improvements precede or depend on others?

Useful evidence may include earlier artifacts, performance and service data, maturity findings,
backlogs, audit results, user research, investment requests, contracts, cost estimates, technical
debt, data profiles, workforce capacity, dependency maps, and lessons from prior initiatives.

## Maturity progression

| Level | What it looks like | Representative evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Recognized** | Teams identify improvements through immediate needs, experience, and available funding opportunities. | Local backlogs, project ideas, issue lists |
| **2 — Described** | Opportunities state expected results, affected work, ownership, dependencies, and indicative measures. | Opportunity briefs, common intake, initial comparisons |
| **3 — Connected** | Priorities trace to outcomes and earlier artifacts and are coordinated across business, information, workforce, and technology changes. | Traceability, dependency map, capability increments |
| **4 — Managed as a portfolio** | Leaders compare value, readiness, risk, capacity, evidence, and results across the improvement portfolio. | Portfolio reviews, decision records, benefit and learning measures |
| **5 — Kept current** | Priorities adapt through measured outcomes, delivery evidence, changing services, and newly available options. | Reprioritization history, retired ideas, outcome reviews |

The target is a transparent and adaptable decision process, not maximum scoring detail. A useful
portfolio can remain concise when its reasoning and dependencies are clear.

## Workshop

### Improvement and prioritization workshop

| Item | Guidance |
|---|---|
| **Participants** | Service and asset leaders, operations, maintenance, engineering, finance, capital planning, performance, workforce, data, architecture, product, security, procurement, and change representatives |
| **Preparation** | Bring prior artifacts, assessment and maturity findings, performance evidence, current initiatives, constraints, cost context, and candidate opportunities |
| **Typical length** | Three hours for initial framing and comparison, followed by evidence refinement |
| **Result** | A reviewed opportunity portfolio with outcomes, dependencies, relative priority, measures, ownership, and next decisions |

Suggested activities:

1. Review the most important outcomes, performance gaps, operating needs, and information findings.
2. Frame opportunities as capability results without prescribing a solution.
3. Group related business, workforce, information, control, and technology changes.
4. Identify prerequisites, dependencies, affected stakeholders, and adoption needs.
5. Compare public value, service impact, urgency, readiness, feasibility, cost range, risk, and learning value.
6. Evaluate AI ideas using where, when, and how questions alongside non-AI alternatives.
7. Agree on relative priority, ownership, measures, evidence to collect, and options requiring technical evaluation.

## What you will produce

The **Prioritized Improvement Portfolio** is the fifth component of the Asset Management Capability
Blueprint. At minimum it contains:

- clearly framed opportunities and expected capability outcomes;
- evidence, affected stakeholders, scope, and indicative measures;
- connections to earlier business artifacts and maturity findings;
- business, workforce, information, control, policy, and technology enablers;
- dependencies, constraints, assumptions, uncertainty, and readiness;
- comparison criteria, relative priority, ownership, and next decision;
- AI suitability and human-accountability considerations where applicable;
- a record of deferred, combined, or retired opportunities and rationale.

The artifact is ready to carry forward when leaders can explain what improvements matter, why they
matter, how they relate, and which solution questions require evaluation.

## Worked example

| Opportunity | Expected result | Important dependencies | Initial direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Establish trusted asset identity across operations, GIS, finance, and projects | Less reconciliation and clearer lifecycle history | Stewardship, matching rules, source decisions, integration capacity | Foundational capability increment |
| Improve capital-project handover | New assets enter service with complete, usable operating and maintenance information | Delivery requirements, acceptance responsibility, document and asset standards | Pilot with a representative capital program |
| Support risk-informed renewal scenarios | Decision-makers can compare service, condition, cost, criticality, and funding implications | Trusted identity, current condition, agreed decision criteria, analytical skills | Sequence after foundational information work |
| Assist staff in finding manuals and summarizing work history | Less time spent locating and reviewing distributed material | Records access, content quality, permissions, evaluation, human review | Explore a bounded AI-assisted use case |
