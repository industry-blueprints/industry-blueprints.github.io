  <h2>The organizing model</h2>
  <p>Six components split evenly across business and technical knowledge, paired one to one: <strong>Workload</strong> (the work) maps to <strong>Reference Architecture</strong> (the landscape); <strong>Capability</strong> (the ability) maps to <strong>Capability Architecture</strong> (the shape); <strong>Use Case</strong> (the opportunity) maps to <strong>Implementation Pattern</strong> (the design). A workload normally has several capabilities and use cases; a capability is reusable across workloads when the underlying ability is substantively the same; a use case may require multiple capabilities.</p>
  <p>Not every component is its own page. Workloads, capabilities, and reference architectures are destinations you can browse to. A use case is a section within its capability's page, its implementation pattern sits inline with that use case, and the capability architecture is the section that draws those patterns together at the end of the same page. The component model describes the knowledge, not the navigation.</p>
  <p>Everything else — AI opportunities, discovery and assessment, data, standards crosswalks, technology and product options, and governance — attaches to these six components as a cross-cutting layer, not as an additional level in the hierarchy. The knowledge base behaves like a graph: relationships are explicit, directional, and drawn from a controlled vocabulary rather than invented per page.</p>
  <h2>Bottom-up composition on the technical side</h2>
  <p>Implementation patterns compose into a capability architecture; capability architectures compose into a workload's reference architecture. A capability architecture can have multiple implementation-pattern variants depending on deployment model, data availability, risk, or product ecosystem. Technology and vendor options are shown only after the vendor-neutral design is understood — a canonical definition never contains a vendor dependency.</p>
  <blockquote>What work do you do? → What must you be able to do? → What opportunity is worth solving? → What should the environment look like? → What shape does that ability take? → How can it be built?</blockquote>
  <h2>Built progressively</h2>
  <p>The library will not pretend that every topic is equally complete. Broad catalog entries establish coverage while a smaller set of lighthouse examples receive practitioner, solution, and evidence depth.</p>
